# UK and non-EU/non-US VAT/GST registration obligations for a foreign (US LLC) seller of digital content (lifetime access to videos) to consumers, as of October 2026

Research date / verification date: 2026-10-12. Scope: indirect tax only (VAT/GST/consumption/service tax), B2C-focused, a US LLC with no establishment abroad. EU and US excluded. Source quality legend used below: **[PRIMARY]** = tax authority / government page fetched; **[STRIPE]** = Stripe Tax docs (vendor, but detailed and per-country, maintained as of 2026); **[SECONDARY]** = adviser/compliance-vendor blog (Kintsugi, Quaderno, Avalara, Fonoa, vatcalc, PayPro Global, etc.), lower reliability. Rates not stated by any fetched source are labelled "rate: not verified (general knowledge / brief)". Most country thresholds below come from Stripe's per-country pages, which are a compliance vendor's summary of local law, not the statute: confirm before registering.

## 1. United Kingdom: VAT on B2C digital services by a non-UK seller

### Takeaway
A non-UK seller with no UK establishment (a "non-established taxable person", NETP) has **no registration threshold**: it must register for UK VAT within 30 days of the first taxable (B2C) sale, or earlier if it expects such a sale within 30 days. B2B sales to UK VAT-registered customers are outside the seller's UK VAT (customer accounts for it), and a seller with only B2B sales cannot/need not register. Returns are generally quarterly and MTD software rules apply to registered businesses; no 2026-specific change was found.

### Cited Findings
- Digital services supplied to UK consumers are liable to UK VAT; a non-UK business with such supplies must register. The place of supply of cross-border digital services is the consumer's location. Supplies to consumers outside the UK are not liable to UK VAT. — [GOV.UK: VAT rules for supplies of digital services to consumers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers) [PRIMARY]
- Evidence of location: the supplier "must get and keep 2 pieces of information as evidence of where a consumer normally lives". For micro/small businesses using a payment service provider, the two pieces can be billing address with country (or phone number with country dialling code) plus the PSP's 2-digit country code; if they match that is enough; if they do not, the supplier must contact the consumer to resolve the discrepancy. Presumptions exist for e.g. mobile-phone supplies (SIM country code). — [GOV.UK digital services](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers) [PRIMARY]
- B2B: a customer cannot force B2B treatment without giving a valid VAT registration number; if the customer gives none, treat the supply as B2C and charge VAT. For valid cross-border business customers the customer accounts for any VAT due. — [GOV.UK digital services](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers) [PRIMARY]
- Platforms/marketplaces: where a supply goes through a third-party digital platform, the platform (not the supplier) is responsible for accounting for the VAT; a role limited to payment processing does not make a business a "digital platform". — [GOV.UK digital services](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers) [PRIMARY]
- No threshold for NETPs: "the registration threshold for taxable supplies does not apply to you"; must register "if you make taxable sales in the UK or expect to do so in the next 30 days"; NETP must notify HMRC within 30 days; Notice 700/1 §2.3 says registration is needed "regardless of their value and including supplies of digital services". — [VAT Notice 700/1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat) [PRIMARY; section quotations as returned by the fetch tool]
- Stripe's reading: a US-based seller of digital services "must register in the UK as soon as you have reasonable grounds to believe that a UK customer will purchase"; if only B2B sales to UK businesses, no need to register because those are reverse-charged and not taxable transactions; sales made only through online marketplaces that account for the VAT do not count and do not require registration. Registration threshold displayed: "1 transaction". — [Stripe Tax: collect tax in the UK](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/europe/collect-tax.md?tax-jurisdiction-europe=united-kingdom) [STRIPE]
- HMRC's internal manual: a business is not entitled/liable to register in the UK if all its UK supplies are subject to the reverse charge (VATREG37250). — [VATREG37250](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37250) [PRIMARY, seen via search listing; content as summarised by search tool]
- HMRC adviser reply (2024 community forum): selling digital services from overseas to individuals in the UK, there is no threshold before the requirement to register. — [HMRC community forum](https://community.hmrc.gov.uk/customerforums/vat/15f99cef-69c1-ee11-a81c-000d3a0d1900) [PRIMARY-ish, informal]
- Registration via HMRC's online VAT registration service; address given should be the principal place of business outside the UK (not a mail-forwarding address); a UK VAT representative/agent is optional ("If you choose not to appoint a VAT representative or agent, you will be responsible for accounting for UK VAT to HMRC"). — [GOV.UK search results summarising Notice 700/1 / VAT1 notes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat) [PRIMARY, via search summary]
- Rate: standard rate 20% applies to electronically supplied services to UK consumers — [SECONDARY: Fonoa UK guide](https://fonoa.com/countries/united-kingdom/tax-on-digital-services) (the GOV.UK page fetched did not state a rate; 20% is the UK standard rate by general knowledge).
- Returns: quarterly is the general rule (monthly on request/for regular repayment positions); the UK provides no simplified return for non-resident companies. — [SECONDARY: Fonoa UK](https://fonoa.com/countries/united-kingdom/tax-on-digital-services), [vatcalc UK](https://www.vatcalc.com/?p=16390); returns/payments due 7th of second month after quarter end per [SECONDARY vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/uk/uk-vat-on-foreign-digital-services/)
- Making Tax Digital: all VAT-registered businesses have had to keep digital records and file via MTD-compatible software since April 2022, unless exempt. — [GOV.UK MTD for VAT collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/making-tax-digital-for-vat) [PRIMARY]. An overseas-trader deferral to 1 Oct 2019 existed but is long passed — [withdrawn GOV.UK MTD page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-how-vat-businesses-and-other-vat-entities-can-get-ready/making-tax-digital-how-vat-businesses-and-other-vat-entities-can-get-ready)
- Territories: Stripe will not calculate UK VAT for BVI, Channel Islands, Falklands, Gibraltar, Isle of Man; Northern Ireland: services follow UK domestic rules (goods have EU-style rules). — [Stripe UK](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/europe/collect-tax.md?tax-jurisdiction-europe=united-kingdom) [STRIPE]
- Notice 700/1 revision note (4 April 2024): NETP guidance updated; voluntary-registration guidance for NETPs removed. — [VAT Notice 700/1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat) [PRIMARY]

### Inferences
- For a small seller the UK is a "register from first B2C sale" jurisdiction (like India, Korea, Saudi); the only legal ways to avoid registering are (a) sell only through a platform/MoR that is the deemed supplier, (b) sell only B2B with valid VAT numbers, or (c) do not sell to UK consumers (geo-block).
- Because the consumer-location rule requires two pieces of non-conflicting evidence and a PSP country code counts, Stripe/Checkout billing country + card-issuing country satisfies the evidence test in practice.

### Gaps
- No 2026 UK change specific to overseas digital sellers was found (searches returned nothing); a negative result, not proof. Not verified: whether HMRC applies MTD to NETPs with any special treatment, currency-conversion rules for non-sterling sales, and the exact penalties. The gov.uk digital services page does not state the rate or return frequency; those come from secondary sources.

## 2. Jurisdiction table: rate, threshold, mechanism, filing, B2B (non-EU, non-US)

### Takeaway
Roughly 12 jurisdictions of the 35 asked about are first-sale (no threshold) for B2C digital services (UK, India, South Korea, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Türkiye, Russia, Kazakhstan, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Uruguay, Mexico, Kenya, Brazil from Aug 2026, and probably Vietnam/Israel/Argentina), and ~15 are threshold-based (Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Australia, NZ, Canada, Japan, Singapore, Taiwan, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Philippines, South Africa, plus Nigeria/Egypt/Ukraine with disputed figures). Almost everywhere B2B digital services are reverse-charged to the customer, so registration is driven by B2C sales; exceptions where B2B also counts/charged: Malaysia, Indonesia, Kenya, South Africa, Nigeria, Mexico, Vietnam, and BC/Manitoba/Saskatchewan provincial sales tax.

## 3. Defer-until-threshold vs register-from-first-sale; geo-blocking vs registering; Merchant-of-Record coverage

### Takeaway
A small seller can defer registration only in threshold jurisdictions and must then monitor rolling totals (Stripe Tax's monitoring tool does this, and for Canada/AU/NZ/NO etc. it counts B2C only). Registering everywhere is impractical; the realistic options are (1) a Merchant of Record (MoR) such as Paddle or Stripe Managed Payments, which becomes the seller for tax in its covered countries, (2) geo-blocking the first-sale countries not covered by the MoR, or (3) registering only in the few countries where actual volume appears. Stripe Managed Payments (a MoR) explicitly covers 80+ countries but not UAE, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Uruguay.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe Tax monitors registration thresholds per country and alerts via email/Dashboard; it only monitors thresholds for sales outside the business's home country; "Always visit a country's government website to verify". — [Stripe APAC](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/asia-pacific.md), [Stripe Africa](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/africa.md) [STRIPE]
- Stripe Tax (calculation/monitoring, not MoR) supports digital products for remote sellers in nearly all jurisdictions in the question, but **Brazil, Argentina and Israel are absent from the Stripe Tax supported-countries table** (Israel is, however, present in the Managed Payments tax-coverage list). — [Stripe supported countries](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries), [Managed Payments tax compliance](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/tax-compliance) [STRIPE]
- **Stripe Managed Payments (MoR)**: handles calculation, collection, registration, filing and remittance for digital products in "more than 80 countries"; cross-border coverage list includes GB, NO, CH, LI, IS, UA, AL, BY, RS, MD, GI; AU, NZ, JP, KR, SG, IN, ID, MY, PH, TH, VN, TW, SA, TR, IL, KZ, HK and others; ZA, KE, NG, EG, CM, GH, UG, ZM, ZW; CA, US, MX, BB, BM, KY, VG; all 27 EU states. **Not on the list**: AE (UAE), BR, AR, CL, CO, PE, UY, RU (restricted). Not relevant at small scale.

### Inferences
- Classification for a small seller selling direct with Stripe Tax (not MoR):
  - *Can realistically defer until threshold* (with monitoring): Norway, Switzerland (CHF 100k worldwide), Iceland, Australia, NZ, Canada federal and Quebec, BC, Japan, Singapore, Taiwan, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Philippines, South Africa, and (disputed) Nigeria, Egypt, Ukraine.
  - *Register from first sale or geo-block:* UK, India, South Korea, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Türkiye, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Vietnam, Mexico, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Uruguay, Brazil (from Aug 2026), Saskatchewan and Manitoba provincial taxes; Israel/Argentina unclear (treat as first-sale risk).
- With a MoR the first-sale/threshold distinction mostly disappears for covered countries; the exposure shifts to (a) countries the MoR does not cover or blocks (UAE, Brazil, LatAm Southern Cone, Russia, China, etc.), where the seller is still liable or must block, and (b) MoR fees.
- Geo-blocking is a legitimate alternative for low-volume first-sale countries; at the payment layer MoRs already refuse their restricted lists (Paddle 23 countries; Managed Payments 9 territories), so the seller's own geo-block list only needs to add countries the MoR allows but does not cover tax for (for Managed Payments: UAE, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Uruguay).
- Using Managed Payments for "lifetime access to streamed videos": the explicit tax-code taxonomy lacks a "streamed, permanent rights" audiovisual code; the closest are the "streamed, limited rights" audiovisual code and on-demand online courses (streamed). Whether Stripe would accept this product type is unconfirmed and should be checked with Stripe before relying on it.

### Gaps
- No Paddle country list of tax registrations or Lemon Squeezy official coverage page was retrieved; Paddle and Lemon Squeezy treatment of Brazil, UAE, and LatAm is unknown. FastSpring, Polar, Gumroad, Creem etc. not researched.
- No data on what happens (liability) for sales into non-covered MoR countries when the MoR declines tax assumption beyond Stripe's statement that the seller must handle compliance.
- No legal source on whether geo-blocking alone (while a customer uses a VPN or foreign card) suffices as a compliance defence in each jurisdiction.

## 4. Sanctions / embargoed countries relevant to selling

### Takeaway
For a US LLC, OFAC rules apply regardless of customer location: Cuba, Iran, North Korea and the Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions of Ukraine are consistently listed as comprehensively sanctioned; Syria's comprehensive program no longer appears in OFAC's active program list. Payment/MoR platforms apply broader blocks (Russia, Belarus, Syria, China for Managed Payments, etc.). No EU-sanctions source was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- OFAC's current program list (34 programs) includes country programs for Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Belarus, Russia-related (Russian Harmful Foreign Activities; Ukraine-/Russia-related), Venezuela, Burma, Libya, Yemen, Somalia, Sudan, South Sudan, Mali, Nicaragua, DRC, CAR, Lebanon, Iraq, Balkans, Afghanistan; **no Syria program** appears (only "Promoting Accountability for Assad and Regional Stabilization", PAARSS). The page does not label programs comprehensive vs selective. — [OFAC programs](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information) [PRIMARY]
- 2026 secondary lists treat **Cuba, Iran, North Korea** as comprehensively sanctioned and the **Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk** regions of Ukraine as comprehensively sanctioned; Russia itself falls under targeted/sectoral programs; sources disagree on Syria (some older lists include it). — [Sanctions Lawyers 2026](https://sanctionslawyers.net/blog-en/ofac-sanctioned-countries-list-2026/), [AIST policy (status July 2026)](https://www.aist.org/About-AIST/Leadership-Governance/Governance-Policies/Commerce-Prohibition-Policy) [SECONDARY]
- Stripe Managed Payments restricted customer countries: Ascension Island, China, Cuba, Iran, Kosovo, North Korea, Russia, Syria, Tristan da Cunha. — [Managed Payments eligibility](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility) [STRIPE]
- Paddle blocks 23 countries/territories: Afghanistan, Netherlands Antilles, Antarctica, Belarus, DR Congo, Central African Republic, Serbia and Montenegro (legacy code), Cuba, Haiti, Iran, North Korea, Libya, Mali, Myanmar, Nicaragua, Russia, Sudan, Somalia, South Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Yemen, Zimbabwe. — [Paddle supported countries](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/sell/supported-countries-locales) [VENDOR PRIMARY]

### Inferences
- Because Russia, Belarus and Syria are blocked by both MoRs and Russia/Belarus are subject to OFAC/EU measures, Russian VAT registration (which Stripe Tax lists as "1 transaction") is moot for a seller using those MoRs; a seller handling payments itself would face sanctions-screening and payment-rail problems rather than VAT being the main issue.
- The practical block list for a US-LLC seller: Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea/DNR/LNR, plus (platform policy) Russia, Belarus, and whatever the chosen payment platform lists. Whether to also block Venezuela, Myanmar, Sudan etc. is a platform-policy rather than universal-embargo question.

### Gaps
- EU sanctions (Council regulations on Russia/Belarus, etc.) and UK sanctions were not researched; no EU or UK government source was retrieved. Syria's present OFAC status was inferred from the absence of a program in the current list, not from an explicit OFAC statement of termination. The OFAC page did not identify which programs prohibit services to individuals (e.g. whether providing online video content falls under information-materials exemptions); legal advice needed.
