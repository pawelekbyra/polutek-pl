## 1. EU/Polish/UK consumer law for "lifetime" digital video access: withdrawal, checkout capture, refunds, conformity, price display, geo-blocking, jurisdiction

### Takeaway
The single most important development is CJEU C-234/25 Sky Österreich (9 July 2026): a streaming offering that is "dynamic" (catalogue updates, recommendations, adaptive features) is a digital SERVICE, not digital content, so the Art. 16(m) "lose withdrawal right once streaming starts" waiver is not available and the consumer keeps the 14-day withdrawal right, subject to proportionate payment if they asked for immediate access. Only a stable, specific body of content (one-off access to identified files/films) is likely still "digital content" where the checkout consent + acknowledgement + durable-medium confirmation route works. A US LLC targeting EU consumers is subject to these mandatory rules via Rome I Art. 6 / Pammer-type "directing" indicia. There is no source-confirmed EU/Polish legal definition of "lifetime"; the risk is unfair-terms/misleading-practice exposure, so the term must be defined in the contract.

### Cited Findings

**A. Withdrawal right and loss of it (core mechanics)**
- Directive 2011/83 Art. 16(m) (original text): withdrawal right does not apply to "the supply of digital content which is not supplied on a tangible medium if the performance has begun" and the consumer gave prior express consent to performance starting and acknowledged losing the right of withdrawal — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/16)
- Art. 8(7): trader must confirm the contract on a durable medium within a reasonable time after conclusion; the confirmation must include the Art. 6(1) information (unless already given on a durable medium) and, where applicable, confirmation of the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement under Art. 16(m) — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/8)
- Art. 14(4)(b): for digital content not on a tangible medium the consumer pays nothing (in full or part) if (i) no prior express consent to start before the 14 days ended, (ii) no acknowledgement at the time of consent, or (iii) the trader did not provide the Art. 7(2)/8(7) confirmation — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/14)
- Art. 14(3) (services): if the consumer expressly asked for performance to begin in the withdrawal period (Art. 8(8)) and then withdraws, they pay an amount proportionate to what has been provided, based on the total contract price (market value if the price is excessive) — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/14); Art. 8(8) requires an "express request" from the consumer for services to start in the period — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/8)
- Art. 10: if the trader omits the Art. 6(1)(h) withdrawal information, the withdrawal period ends 12 months after the original period; if given within those 12 months, 14 days after receipt — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/10)
- Art. 13: refund of all payments without undue delay and in any event within 14 days of learning of the withdrawal, via the same payment method, no fee — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/13)
- Art. 6(1) pre-contract information relevant to digital video: (a) main characteristics, (b)-(c) trader identity/geographic address/phone/email, (e) total price incl. taxes, (g) payment/performance arrangements, (h) withdrawal conditions + model withdrawal form, (j) liability to pay if consumer withdraws after requesting early performance, (k) where no withdrawal right exists or when it is lost, (o) duration / termination conditions for open-ended contracts, (r) functionality incl. technical protection measures, (s) interoperability, (t) out-of-court redress mechanism the trader is subject to — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/6) (original 2011 lettering; Omnibus added (ea)/(ec) etc., see section D)

**B. C-234/25 Sky Österreich Fernsehen (9 July 2026): streaming = digital service when dynamic**
- Court's answer: a streaming service by which the customer accesses, via hyperlink or app, data stored on a server to view live, on demand, or offline after download "does not constitute the supply of digital content but rather ... a digital service, where the offering is of a dynamic nature which goes beyond the mere stable and, as the case may be, continuous provision of specific content" — [CJEU press release 97/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-07/cp260097en.pdf)
- Indicators: offering adapts to individual behaviour/expectations on the basis of monitoring of accessed content, playlists or favourites lists, or influences how the customer uses it, e.g. by recommending content; the distinction depends on the degree of the provider's involvement — [CJEU press release 97/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-07/cp260097en.pdf)
- Facts the Court found pointing to a service: great variety of content in subscriptions tailored to profiles, dedicated access infrastructure, generally updated offering, personal recommendations, user not offered "mere one-off access to a specific piece or pieces of content", content "adjusted continuously", technical features influencing use — [CJEU press release 97/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-07/cp260097en.pdf)
- Consequence: the right of withdrawal "cannot be excluded"; a customer who withdraws after asking for performance to begin in the withdrawal period must pay appropriate compensation, "in principle" calculated on the period of use but possibly on the economic value of content viewed — [CJEU press release 97/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-07/cp260097en.pdf)
- The Court's classification is "subject to verification" by the Austrian Supreme Court; the official judgment is dated 9 July 2026 and the OJ publication is reported as 7 Sept 2026 — [Lexology/Covington summary via insideprivacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/cjeu-decides-when-streaming-subscriptions-are-subject-to-the-right-of-withdrawal/); [EU Law Live](https://eulawlive.com/court-of-justice-streaming-providers-cannot-exclude-consumers-right-of-withdrawal-where-digital-service-is-designed-to-adapt-to-the-users-behaviour/)
- Where in doubt "the rules applicable to services should apply" (as reported by Covington; not verified in the judgment text) — [insideprivacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/cjeu-decides-when-streaming-subscriptions-are-subject-to-the-right-of-withdrawal/)
- Practitioner reading: digital content = identifiable data supplied once that does not change with use (examples: e-book, music album download, "one-off access to a particular film"); the line blurs as the offering becomes personalised, frequently updated and provider-shaped; lifetime access was NOT discussed — [E+H Rechtsanwälte](https://www.eh.at/en/cjeu-on-the-right-of-withdrawal-for-streaming-subscriptions-what-providers-need-to-review-now/)
- AG Szpunar (opinion 26 Feb 2026) had reasoned similarly: digital content is one-off supply, digital services involve ongoing trader involvement; Art. 16(m) must be read strictly; Art. 14(3) protects the trader; relied on PE Digital C-641/19 — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/world-consumer-rights-day-part-2-reshaping-online-subscription-rights-the-sky-austria-case/)
- If a service, the exception for losing withdrawal is Art. 16(a)/(b)-type "fully performed" (per William Fry's reading of the AG opinion), so a service that is never "fully performed" within 14 days keeps the right — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/world-consumer-rights-day-part-2-reshaping-online-subscription-rights-the-sky-austria-case/)

**C. What the checkout must capture (digital-content route) and how**
- Commission guidance (2014): consent/acknowledgement requires a "positive action" (e.g. tick-box); a pre-ticked box or reference to general T&Cs is likely insufficient; consent and acknowledgement can be obtained at the same time — [Reed Smith summary of 2014 guidance](https://reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2014/06/how-new-eu-guidance-on-the-consumer-rights-directi). The 2021 Commission Notice 2021/C 525/01 is the current guidance — [vLex copy](https://eu.vlex.com/vid/commission-notice-guidance-on-1036824691); [e-Justice](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/e-justice/639/EN/consumer_rights_directive_201183) (I could not retrieve its text)
- Polish law (ustawa o prawach konsumenta, art. 38 ust. 1 pkt 13): withdrawal excluded for paid digital content not on a physical medium only if (1) trader began performance with the consumer's express prior consent, (2) consumer was told beforehand that they lose the right after performance and acknowledged it, (3) trader delivered the confirmation under art. 15 ust. 1-2 or art. 21 ust. 1 (durable medium) — [Standardy Prawa art. 38](https://standardyprawa.pl/akt/51/art/9883)
- UK Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 reg. 37: trader may not start supplying digital content in the cancellation period unless consumer gives express consent AND acknowledges loss of right; consumer pays nothing if consent/acknowledgement/confirmation (reg. 12(5)/16(3)) missing — [legislation.gov.uk reg. 37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37)
- UOKiK practice (Poland): in the WSKZ/Varsovia university cases UOKiK treated acceptance of terms as NOT being the required separate, express consent to start performance before the withdrawal period ends; penalties up to 10% of turnover — [prawo.pl](https://www.prawo.pl/biznes/uokik-z-zarzutami-w-sprawie-praktyk-uczelni-niepublicznych,1549189.html)
- Polish commentary: for an online course classified as a digital service, the seller must show the whole course was used to exclude withdrawal (2023 post, author flags uncertainty) — [Poradnik Przedsiębiorcy](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-zwrot-kursu-online-czy-mozna-dokonac-jego-zwrotu)
- CJEU Sofatutor C-565/22 (5 Oct 2023): withdrawal right for an initially-free auto-extended subscription is in principle given only once; a new right arises if the consumer was not clearly informed it would become chargeable/total price — [CJEU press release 154/23](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-10/cp230154en.pdf)

**D. New "withdrawal button" (Art. 11a) – in force 19 June 2026**
- Directive (EU) 2023/2673 inserts Art. 11a in 2011/83: for distance contracts concluded via an online interface with a statutory withdrawal right, an electronic withdrawal function must be continuously available during the withdrawal period; initiation button "withdraw from contract here" (or unambiguous equivalent), confirmation button "confirm withdrawal"; then acknowledgement of receipt on a durable medium restating content and date/time; applies from 19 June 2026; Germany transposed via § 356a BGB — [Hogan Lovells (HLC) note](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-consumer-protection-law-update-new-mandatory-withdrawal-button-what-online-traders-need-to-know)
- Commentary says non-EU traders directing activities at EU consumers are in scope; vendor summaries are not the legal text — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-consumer-protection-law-update-new-9377759/)
- Poland: reported as not having transposed in time (deadline 19 Dec 2025 for adoption, 19 June 2026 for application); the UOKiK-prepared draft UC82 was withdrawn from the Standing Committee in May 2026 after the PM revoked UOKiK's authorisation — [Infor.pl](https://www.infor.pl/prawo/konsument-i-umowy/7612423,zwroty-online-od-19-czerwca-2026-r-na-nowych-zasadach-co-zmienia-dyrektywa-ue-20232673.html); [Akademia LTCA](https://akademialtca.pl/blog/przycisk-zwrotu-w-e-commerce-dyrektywa-ue-2023-2673-jako-nowe-zrodlo-ryzyka-operacyjnego-i-prawnego-dla-e-sklepow); [ITwiz](https://itwiz.pl/nowe-zasady-zwrotow-w-e-commerce-co-sklepy-internetowe-musza-zrobic-przed-19-czerwca-2026-roku/) (these were read via search summaries; status to be re-checked)

**E. "Lifetime access": conformity, updates, modification, termination, unfair terms**
- Directive 2019/770 Art. 8(2): trader must supply updates (incl. security) needed to keep content in conformity; for continuous supply for the contract period; for a single act or series of acts, "for the period of time that the consumer may reasonably expect" given type/purpose/circumstances — [legislation.gov.uk 2019/770 Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/770/article/8)
- Art. 11: single act of supply — liable for non-conformity existing at supply (national limitation must be at least 2 years); continuous supply — liable for non-conformity occurring or becoming apparent within the period the content is to be supplied — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/770/article/11)
- Art. 19 (continuous supply): trader may modify beyond conformity needs only if contract allows and gives a valid reason, no extra cost, consumer clearly informed; if the change negatively impacts access/use (more than minor) the consumer may terminate free of charge and must be informed in advance on a durable medium — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/770/article/19/2020-01-31); [Revista Electrónica de Direito summary](https://cij.up.pt/en/red/previous-editions/2021-nordm-2/modifications-on-the-digital-content-or-digital-service-by-the-trader-in-the-directive-eu-2019770)
- Polish implementation: art. 43k (conformity incl. availability of updates; consumer's reasonable expectations), art. 43l (two-year liability for one-off/part supply; continuous supply = whole supply period), art. 43p (changes only with contractual reason, no cost, clear info; reported as not permitted for one-off supply) — [lexlege art. 43k](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-prawach-konsumenta/art-43k/); [Lege Artis](https://czasopismo.legeartis.org/2022/12/dostarczanie-tresci-cyfrowej-uslugi-cyfrowej-ustawa-prawach-konsmenta/) (art. 43p detail came from a search summary; verify)
- Commission answer of 16 June 2026 on discontinuation of video games: no legal obligation to keep content available after support ends; existing law is the route: pre-contract information on duration/termination (CRD), unfair terms (UCTD), UCPD (describing a limited/revocable licence as something consumers "buy" or "own" may be misleading), Digital Content Directive remedies incl. proportionate refund; Commission to open an end-of-life code-of-conduct dialogue by end 2026 and report on 2019/770 application by year-end — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/eu-declines-video-game-playability-mandate/)
- No UOKiK decision, CJEU case, or German court ruling specifically on "lifetime access"/"Lebenszeit" for digital content was found in several searches (see Gaps).
- Digital Fairness Act: Commission proposal expected Q4 2026 (not yet law; adoption unlikely before late 2027); expected scope: dark patterns, subscriptions/cancellation, personalisation, dynamic pricing — [AKD](https://www.akd.eu/insights/the-digital-fairness-act-what-to-expect-from-the-forthcoming-eu-legislative-proposal-on-strengthening-consumer-protection-rules-for-the-digital-age); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/interface/2025/predictions-2026/digital-fairness-act-and-digital-omnibus)

**F. Price display, Omnibus, personalised pricing**
- Omnibus (2019/2161) added Art. 6(1)(ea) CRD: disclose where the price was personalised on the basis of automated decision-making (distance/off-premises contracts) — [Baker McKenzie](https://globallitigationnews.bakermckenzie.com/2024/10/15/european-union-the-cjeu-confirms-that-a-price-reduction-must-be-calculated-based-on-the-lowest-price-in-the-last-30-days/) (exact wording not retrieved)
- Price Indication Directive 98/6 Art. 6a: price reduction announcements must show the "prior price" = lowest price applied in at least the previous 30 days; CJEU C-330/23 Aldi Süd (26 Sept 2024) confirmed the percentage/promotional reduction must be computed from that prior price — [Baker McKenzie](https://globallitigationnews.bakermckenzie.com/2024/10/15/european-union-the-cjeu-confirms-that-a-price-reduction-must-be-calculated-based-on-the-lowest-price-in-the-last-30-days/)
- Price shown must be total price including taxes (Art. 6(1)(e)) — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/6)

**G. Geo-blocking Regulation 2018/302**
- Art. 4(1)(b): no different general conditions of access for reasons of nationality/residence/establishment for electronically supplied services OTHER THAN services whose main feature is access to/use of copyright-protected works, "including the selling of copyright protected works ... in an intangible form" — [Reg. 2018/302 Art. 4 (adopted text)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2018/302/article/4/adopted)
- Art. 4(2): traders may offer different general conditions, including net sale prices, between Member States or to specific territories/customer groups on a non-discriminatory basis — [Reg. 2018/302 Art. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2018/302/article/4/adopted)
- Art. 1(3): Regulation does not apply to activities in Art. 2(2) of the Services Directive 2006/123 — [Reg. 2018/302 Art. 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2018/302/article/1/adopted); sources state audiovisual services (incl. VoD) are excluded entirely — [Malta Communications Authority](https://mca.org.mt/node/6514); [JRC](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC120267/JRC120267_01.pdf)
- For non-audiovisual copyright content only the access-to-interface (Art. 3) and non-discrimination of payment means (Art. 5) provisions apply (JRC reading) — [JRC](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC120267/JRC120267_01.pdf); one commentator notes ambiguity between the text and Commission press statements — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/geo-blocking-regulation-what-does-it-mean-for-your-business/)

**H. Applicability to a non-EU seller; jurisdiction**
- Rome I Art. 6(1) consumer protection applies where the trader pursues or directs commercial activity to the consumer's country of habitual residence; CJEU Pammer/Alpenhof (C-585/08, C-144/09) lists non-exhaustive indicia of "directing" (international nature of activity, local-language/currency, local phone code etc.); mere website accessibility is not enough — [Conflict of Laws .net](https://conflictoflaws.net/2010/a-g-opinion-on-pammer-and-hotel-alpenhof/?print=pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pammer_and_Alpenhof_cases)
- Under Art. 6(2) a choice of law (e.g. Wyoming law) cannot remove the consumer's mandatory protections of their habitual-residence law (research agent's statement from general knowledge, flagged as not verified in these sources; see Gaps)

**I. ODR / ADR / trader identification / accessibility**
- EU ODR platform closed 20 July 2025 (Reg. 2024/3228 repealed Reg. 524/2013): the platform-link obligation ended; Art. 21 DSA out-of-court mechanisms remain; ADR Directive 2013/11 is being revised; list of ADR bodies: consumer-redress.ec.europa.eu — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/01/abolition-of-the-odr-platform)
- Art. 6(1)(b)-(d) still require trader identity, geographic address, phone/email and complaints address, and Art. 6(1)(t) info on any out-of-court mechanism the trader is subject to — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/6)
- European Accessibility Act (applies from 28 June 2025) covers e-commerce services and audiovisual media services access; microenterprises providing services (fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet ≤ EUR 2m) are exempt; location of seller is not the trigger — [CCPC (Ireland)](https://www.ccpc.ie/business/enforcement/accessibility/european-accessibility-act-guidelines-for-microenterprises/)

### Inferences
- The product ("lifetime access to a creator's catalogue" with premieres, new uploads, "recommended next" or personalised features, comments) fits the Court's "dynamic offering" indicators far more than "one-off access to a specific piece"; the safe planning assumption is Art. 16(a)-style service treatment: 14-day withdrawal right NOT excludable by checkbox, with proportionate compensation if immediate access was requested. The digital-content waiver should be treated as an optional extra, usable only if the product is genuinely a closed, stable set of identified videos (no personalisation, no continuing additions).
- A stable bundle (e.g. a fixed course/film set, downloadable) is where Art. 16(m) still works; it needs three artefacts logged per order: separate un-pre-ticked consent+acknowledgement action, timestamp/IP/version of the text, and the durable-medium confirmation email (otherwise Art. 14(4)(b) = consumer pays nothing, and Art. 10 extends withdrawal up to 12 months+14 days if withdrawal info missing).
- Design pattern that hedges: (1) separate checkbox "I want access to start immediately and understand I must pay a proportionate amount if I withdraw within 14 days" (Art. 6(1)(j), 8(8), 14(3)); (2) optional second statement for any stable downloadable component (Art. 16(m)); (3) durable-medium confirmation with withdrawal info + model form; (4) Art. 11a withdrawal function reachable in account and via confirmation email; (5) a refund path within 14 days (Art. 13).
- "Lifetime" is not defined in any source found; the legally safer drafting is "access for the duration the service is operated" with a stated minimum (e.g. N years), end-of-life notice/refund mechanics, modification clause meeting Art. 19 (valid reason, no extra cost, notice, free termination), and no "own/buy forever" language (UCPD/UCTD risk flagged by the Commission in June 2026). A one-off price with indefinite access probably counts as continuous supply for an indefinite period, so non-conformity liability (Art. 11(3)) and Art. 19 apply for as long as access is offered (my inference, not a cited holding).
- Geo-blocking: for audiovisual content the Regulation likely does not oblige the seller to serve every EU country and permits per-country prices (Art. 4(2) is permissive anyway), but a payment-method discrimination and interface-blocking analysis should be done; blocking by sanctions/VAT/tax-registration reasons is a separate (not geo-blocking) justification.
- With the Polish button transposition missing, the EU rule still applies as an obligation on the trader in cross-border sales where a Member State law applies; the safe plan is to implement the button for all EU users (and for Germany it is already national law).
- Because the LLC directs activity (Polish/English UI, EUR/PLN prices, EU card payments) at EU consumers, EU/Polish mandatory consumer law, UOKiK/other national authorities and consumer-court jurisdiction (Brussels I-recast Art. 17-18, not researched) should be assumed to apply; cross-border enforcement against a US LLC is practically harder but chargebacks and payment-processor policy enforce it.

## 2. Invoices/receipts, record retention, and GDPR for purchase data

### Takeaway
A VAT invoice must carry the Art. 226 particulars (sequential number, supplier's name/address/VAT ID, rate, taxable amount, VAT amount, etc.); for a non-EU seller using the VAT non-Union scheme, invoicing rules of the Member State of identification apply and records must be kept 10 years from end of year. Under GDPR a US LLC offering services to EU consumers needs a lawful basis, an Art. 27 EU representative (unless the narrow exemption applies), and Stripe's DPA/DPF coverage; the owner living and managing in Poland creates a potential "establishment" question.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 226 VAT Directive invoice particulars: date of issue; sequential invoice number; supplier VAT ID; customer VAT ID where customer owes VAT (B2B reverse charge/intra-EU goods); full name and address of supplier and customer; quantity/type of services; date of supply if different; taxable amount per rate and unit price excl. VAT; VAT rate; VAT amount payable (unless a special scheme excludes it); exemption reference/"reverse charge" where relevant — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 226](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/226)
- Art. 219a: invoicing rules follow the Member State where the supply is deemed made, with derogations; for suppliers using a special scheme in Chapter 6 of Title XII (non-Union/Union OSS) the rules of the Member State of identification apply (as read from Art. 219a; "confirm against official text") — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 219a](https://legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/219a?view=plain)
- Non-Union scheme (Art. 369): records sufficiently detailed for the Member State of consumption to verify the return, available electronically on request to the Member State of identification and of consumption, kept 10 years from the end of the year of the transaction — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 369](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/369)
- Union scheme (Art. 369k) has the same 10-year period from 31 December of the transaction year — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 369k](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/369k/data.html)
- A 2025 Council directive amends the OSS chapter headings and adds Arts. 369xa-369xk (some provisions from 1 July 2028) — [NWB database annotation via search](https://datenbank.nwb.de/Dokument/452295_219a) (not verified)
- GDPR Art. 27: where Art. 3(2) applies (offering goods/services to people in the Union), a controller not established in the Union must designate in writing a representative in the Union, established in a Member State where the data subjects are located; exemptions: occasional low-risk processing without large-scale special-category data, or public authorities; designation does not prevent action against the controller — [GDPR Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/)
- EDPB Guidelines 3/2018 explain the establishment (Art. 3(1)) and targeting (Art. 3(2)) tests and the Art. 27 representative — [EDPB guidelines](https://edpb.europa.eu/sites/edpb/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_3_2018_territorial_scope_en.pdf)
- Stripe is controller for some activities and processor when providing services to businesses; merchant keeps its own GDPR obligations (privacy notices, data-subject rights); the DPA counterparty is Stripe, LLC for Americas-located users and Stripe Payments Europe Ltd otherwise; Stripe, LLC is certified under EU-US/UK/Swiss DPF with fallback SCCs — [Stripe DPA](https://stripe.com/legal/dpa); [Stripe Privacy Center](https://stripe.com/legal/privacy-center); [Stripe DPF policy](https://stripe.com/legal/data-privacy-framework); [Stripe DPA FAQ](https://stripe.com/legal/dpa/faqs)

## 3. Payments risk: chargebacks, fraud, SCA/3DS, sanctions, reserves, restricted categories

### Takeaway
Stripe will review the account at activation; ordinary (non-adult, non-third-party-marketplace) video/digital content is not on the restricted list, but "lifetime access" one-time payments are chargeback-prone, and liability shift via 3DS is conditional. Sanctions screening applies because the seller is a US entity (a US person for OFAC); the practical block-list includes comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe cannot work with adult content/services; content-creation platforms that host third-party creators' content and take payments for creators need pre-approval, whereas a platform where creators sell their own content from their own sites does not; list is "representative but not exhaustive" and each account is reviewed at activation — [Stripe restricted businesses FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-business-list-faqs)
- Stripe triggers 3DS where required by SCA/PSD2; successful 3DS usually moves fraud-dispute liability to the issuer but not guaranteed; frictionless exemptions and fallback charges lack the liability shift; high-fraud accounts in monitoring programs can lose protection — [Stripe 3DS flow](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/3d-secure/authentication-flow); [Stripe 3DS2 guide](https://stripe.com/guides/3d-secure-2); [Stripe monitoring program FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/monitoring-program-faq)
- Dispute fee per dispute (US$15 or local equivalent in the Help Center excerpt), returned only if the dispute is won ("countered" fee); auto-represented disputes still count toward network monitoring totals; digital-goods responses must show what was ordered, how delivered, to whom — [Stripe how disputes work](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/how-disputes-work); [Stripe dispute best practices](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/prevention/best-practices)
- Stripe may apply rolling reserves (a percentage of each transaction held for a window, e.g. 30 days) based on account risk; no Stripe source found that automatically reserves new digital-goods accounts — [Stripe reserves FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/reserves-frequently-asked-questions)
- Stripe Radar rules (including a has_liability_shift attribute) are available for fraud screening — [Stripe Radar rules](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/rules)
- OFAC lists country/region programmes with 'last updated' dates in Sept-Oct 2026 incl. Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Belarus, Russia-related, Ukraine-/Russia-related, Venezuela, Burma, Libya; no standalone Syria programme listed on that page (a "PAARSS" programme is) — [OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information)

### Inferences
- OFAC applies to US persons including US-formed LLCs wherever the owner lives (general principle; the OFAC page fetched did not state it): so the LLC should screen and refuse sales to comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions (Cuba, Iran, North Korea; Crimea/DNR/LNR regions; Syria status to be verified), SDN-listed names, and avoid payouts to such parties; Stripe itself restricts unsupported countries (not researched).
- Free-trial/instant-access product plus 14-day withdrawal right plus "lifetime" marketing means high "friendly fraud" and "service not as described" disputes; mitigations: clear statement descriptor, receipt email, visible refund policy, proactive 14-day refunds (cheaper than a dispute fee and ratio hit), 3DS for EU cards (SCA is mandatory anyway), Radar rules, velocity limits, account-sharing detection.
- Start with low payout expectations: a new account with low history can see reserves or payout delays (Stripe explains the mechanism but not specific triggers); keep documentation (product description, terms, delivery logs) ready for the activation review.

## 4. US LLC administration for a foreign-owned single-member Wyoming LLC (flag level)

### Takeaway
Foreign-owned US single-member LLCs must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year (paper/fax only; USD 25,000 penalty for failure), keep a Wyoming registered agent and file the annual report/licence tax ($60 minimum) or be dissolved; FinCEN BOI reporting no longer applies to US-formed companies (final rule August 2026). Banking and Stripe are achievable for non-resident owners but are KYC-heavy and rely on third-party guidance. The "US LLC managed from Poland" mismatch must go to a Polish tax adviser.

### Cited Findings
- Form 5472 (rev. Dec 2024; IRS page reviewed 30 Apr 2026): reporting corporations include foreign-owned US disregarded entities (DEs), treated as corporations for section 6038A; filed with a pro forma Form 1120 showing name/address and items B and E, marked "Foreign-owned U.S. DE"; due on the Form 1120 due date (extension via Form 7004 by the regular due date); e-filing is not permitted for foreign-owned US DEs (fax 855-887-7737 or mail to Ogden, UT); reportable transactions include monetary transactions with foreign related parties (Part IV) and, for DEs only, other transactions such as formation/dissolution/acquisition (Part V) — [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)
- Penalty: USD 25,000 for failure to file/maintain records; additional USD 25,000 if failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notice, plus further amounts per 30-day period; criminal penalties may apply; permanent books and records required — [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)
- Practitioner note: the filing obligation does not depend on income; owner capital contributions/loans are commonly cited triggers; a case of a USD 25,000 notice for tax year 2023 is described — [SDO CPA](https://www.sdocpa.com/form-5472-foreign-owned-business-filing/) (secondary source)
- FinCEN BOI: interim final rule 26 March 2025 removed reporting for US companies/US persons; final rule (issued about 11 Aug 2026, published 14 Aug 2026, effective 14 Aug 2026) keeps domestic entities outside the "reporting company" definition; only certain foreign entities registered to do business in the US report — [FinCEN BOI](https://www.fincen.gov/boi); [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/boi-final-rule-confirms-us-company-reporting-exemption/); [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-338672); [Sidley](https://whitecollarwatch.sidley.com/category/corporate-transparency-act/) (issuance date differs across sources: 11 vs 13/14 Aug)
- Wyoming: annual report due on the first day of the anniversary month of formation (file up to 120 days early); licence tax is the greater of USD 60 or USD 0.0002 per dollar of assets located and employed in Wyoming; delinquent from the 2nd day of the next month; administratively dissolved if not filed within 60 days after the due date; reinstatement generally only within two years — [Wyoming SOS FAQ](https://sos.wyo.gov/faqs.aspx?root=BUS)
- Registered agent: articles must state the registered office and agent, with the agent's written consent; a lapsed agent is a reinstatement/penalty issue (SOS form lists a USD 250 penalty plus USD 100 reinstatement fee) — [Wyoming Registered Offices and Agents Act (Ch. 28)](https://sos.wyo.gov/Forms/WyoBiz/Registered_Offices_and_Agents_Act_Chapter_28.pdf); [SOS LLC reinstatement form](https://sos.wyo.gov/Forms/Business/LLC/LLC-CertificateReinstatement.pdf); the SOS FAQ notes fee schedules changed over time — [SOS fee schedule](https://sos.wyo.gov/business/docs/businessfees.pdf)
- Stripe Atlas: USD 500 one-time incl. first-year registered agent (USD 100/yr after), offers C-corp, LLC and subsidiary options, Delaware incorporation, a Stripe Treasury bank account (Fifth Third Bank, N.A.); page does not mention Wyoming or non-US eligibility; a Poland Atlas page exists — [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas)
- Non-US owner of a US LLC opening Stripe: third-party guides say you need EIN, a US bank account, passport/ID, business documents matching EIN name, and warn about mismatches and prohibited industries; EIN for non-SSN founders via paper SS-4 (1-3 weeks) — [Terms.law](https://terms.law/Invest-USA/bank-compliance/stripe-for-foreign-llc.html); [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/how-to-open-a-stripe-account-for-a-us-llc-as-a-non-resident-founder) (secondary; confirm with Stripe)
- Mercury: reported to open accounts for US LLCs with non-US founders (EIN needed, passport accepted, owners ≥25% need ID, no PO box/registered-agent address as principal place of business, longer KYC 2-3 weeks, prohibited-country list) — [Private Law Wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/mercury); [StartupOwl](https://startupowl.com/start/us-business-bank-account-non-resident) (secondary; sources disagree on the country list; no official Mercury page retrieved)

### Inferences
- Annual calendar for the LLC: Wyoming annual report + licence tax (anniversary month), registered agent renewal, Form 5472 + pro forma 1120 (fax/mail) with Form 7004 if needed, EIN/SS-4 at start, books and records (10+ year retention aligns with VAT), no BOI report (as of Aug 2026 final rule, but re-check; the Corporate Transparency Act itself was not repealed).
- Any owner funding, loans, draws or payments between owner and LLC are reportable related-party transactions; plan to record every transfer between the owner (Polish resident) and the LLC and keep a ledger from day one.
- Mismatch flag: a Wyoming LLC with a single owner resident in Poland who manages it from Poland (place of effective management / "real seat", possible permanent-establishment, CFC or tax-transparency questions in Poland, potential GDPR "establishment" and consumer-law "establishment" arguments) is a structural risk that requires a Polish tax adviser and (for corporate/consumer law) a Polish lawyer; not analysed here by instruction. Banks and Stripe may also challenge an LLC with no US presence (Mercury's address rule; Shopify forum reports of proof-of-operations demands).
- Practical choice among Stripe-direct, Paddle/MoR or Stripe Managed Payments materially changes who is the seller of record for VAT, invoices, withdrawal and refunds; the MoR path reduces the LLC's consumer-law/VAT surface but limits control of the checkout (consent wording is set by the MoR's flow plus the seller's own terms).

### Gaps
- No IRS or Wyoming source for EIN issuance to foreign owners without SSN in 2026 (the IRS SS-4 page was not fetched); fax/phone/paper procedure came from third-party guides.
- Whether IRS permits e-filing of Form 5472 for DEs after the Dec 2024 revision (IRS page says no; recheck before filing).

## 5. Legal documents the site needs (terms, refund policy, withdrawal text, privacy, licence scope)

### Takeaway
The pre-contract information duties and the checkout/confirmation requirements in sections 1-2 translate into a defined set of documents and checkout artefacts; no source defines a standard "lifetime access" licence, so scope, transferability and account-sharing terms must be written out and kept fair under UCTD/art. 385 KC (Polish Civil Code, unfair-terms rules).

### Cited Findings
- Art. 6(1) CRD information list (identity, address, price incl. taxes, withdrawal conditions and model form, early-performance cost liability, loss of withdrawal, duration/termination, functionality/DRM, interoperability, redress) is the minimum content of terms of sale and checkout page — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/6)
- Durable-medium confirmation must restate the Art. 6(1) information and (digital content route) the consent/acknowledgement — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/8)
- Art. 11a withdrawal function and acknowledgement-of-receipt email (from 19 June 2026) — [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-consumer-protection-law-update-new-mandatory-withdrawal-button-what-online-traders-need-to-know)
- Digital-content modification clause must satisfy Art. 19 (contractual reason, no cost, clear notice, free termination) — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/770/article/19/2020-01-31)
- Unfair-term and misleading-practice exposure for EULA terms on access and discontinuation, and for "buy"/"own" language for revocable licences — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/eu-declines-video-game-playability-mandate/)
- Paddle (as MoR) states its own buyer terms/refund policy override seller policy where statutory rights apply, and its handbook recommends sellers list a refund policy (30-day money-back is advised voluntarily) — [Paddle refund policy](https://www.paddle.com/legal/refund-policy); [Paddle digital products](https://developer.paddle.com/get-started/how-paddle-works/digital-products/)

### Inferences
- Document set: (1) Terms of Sale/Service in Polish and English (and other languages served) with seller identification (LLC, Wyoming address, contact, VAT ID), product description, total price incl. VAT, payment, delivery by access activation, duration/"lifetime" definition and termination, modification clause (Art. 19), conformity/updates statement (2019/770; Polish art. 43k-43p), liability limits that do not exclude statutory rights; (2) Withdrawal information + model withdrawal form + in-account withdrawal button; (3) Checkout statements: express request for immediate access (Art. 8(8)/6(1)(j)), and where applicable the Art. 16(m) consent+acknowledgement; (4) Refund policy: statutory 14 days (refund within 14 days, same payment method), plus any voluntary extension; (5) Privacy policy updated for purchases/Stripe/invoicing/retention/transfer/Art. 27 representative; (6) Licence terms: personal, non-transferable, non-sublicensable access right; no download/redistribution (no DRM claim that overstates; not "ownership"); account sharing limits stated plainly with technical enforcement proportionate; consequences for breach and for account deletion; what happens at service end-of-life (notice period, refund/pro-rata or offline download), (7) Cookie/telemetry notice if applicable (project already runs cookie-less telemetry), (8) Accessibility statement if not exempt, (9) ADR/complaints info and trader complaint address.
- Transferability/inheritance: no EU source found requiring transferability; given unfair-terms law, a non-transferable personal licence is defensible if clearly disclosed; account sharing limits should be reasonable and disclosed pre-purchase (Art. 6(1)(r) functionality/usage restrictions).
- Governing law/jurisdiction clauses selecting Wyoming courts or arbitration against EU consumers are likely unenforceable/unfair (UCTD; Rome I Art. 6(2)); safer: consumer's mandatory law preserved and consumer court access stated.
- Keep versions of all documents with hashes/timestamps and store the accepted version per order (supports burden of proof for consent and for UOKiK/chargeback defence).

- No authoritative EU text on transferability, resale or account sharing for streaming subscriptions/lifetime licences (the Commission declined a playability mandate; DFA not drafted).
