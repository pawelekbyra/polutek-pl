# EU VAT obligations of a Wyoming single-member LLC selling lifetime (one-time) access to pre-recorded video, B2C and B2B, worldwide (state of play: 12 October 2026)

Scope: VAT only (no CIT/PIT, no US tax). Not tax advice. Two scenarios are used throughout:
- **Scenario A** = the LLC has NO business establishment and NO fixed establishment in the EU (non-Union OSS applies).
- **Scenario B** = the LLC's VAT "place of business" is in Poland because its sole owner, a Polish tax resident, manages it from Poland (Union OSS + Polish VAT registration). **This is a flag for a Polish tax adviser (doradca podatkowy).**

Source-quality note for the report writer: the European Commission OSS guide (revised July 2026, applicable 1 Jan 2027) and the Commission TBE Explanatory Notes are primary/near-primary. Article texts of Directive 2006/112 and Regulation 282/2011 were read from legislation.gov.uk, which is a frozen copy of EUR-Lex as of 31 Dec 2020 (so it does NOT contain amendments that took effect after that date, e.g. the July 2021 OSS reforms; I used it only for provisions that did not change). EUR-Lex itself could not be fetched. Page content was read through a summarising fetch tool; wording of quotes should be re-checked against EUR-Lex before publication.

## 1. Are lifetime-access pre-recorded videos "electronically supplied services" (ESS)? Place of supply B2C and B2B, evidence and VIES

### Takeaway
Yes: on-demand video catalogues, downloads/streams of films and fully automated e-learning are listed ESS (Reg. 282/2011 Art. 7 + Annex I). B2C: taxed where the consumer is established/resides (worldwide supplier, no threshold in Scenario A). B2B: taxed at the business customer's seat (reverse charge by customer for EU customers; outside EU VAT scope for non-EU customers). Courses with a live teacher are NOT ESS.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: ESS are services delivered over the Internet/electronic network, "essentially automated and involving minimal human intervention, and impossible to ensure in the absence of information technology" (Art. 7(1) Reg. 282/2011). — [Commission TBE Explanatory Notes (Annex reproducing Art. 7)](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf); Art. 7 also at [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Art. 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/7)
- Annex I (indicative list) point 4 covers downloading/accessing films, music and games, "on-demand TV or video services selected from a catalogue by the media service provider", and "audio and audiovisual content not provided under a media service provider's editorial responsibility"; point 5 covers automated distance teaching "that depends on the internet and needs little or no human intervention", including virtual classrooms and automatically marked online workbooks, except where the internet is "simply" a communication tool between teacher and student. — [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Annex I](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/annex/I); older wording in [TBE Explanatory Notes](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Art. 7(3) excludes from ESS, among others: "teaching services, where the course content is delivered by a teacher over the Internet ... (namely via a remote link)", telephone helpdesk, services of professionals advising by e-mail, DVDs/CDs, and tickets/accommodation booked online. — [TBE Explanatory Notes, Art. 7 text](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- B2C place of supply: for telecom, broadcasting and electronic services supplied to non-taxable persons it is the Member State where the customer is established, has permanent address or usually resides. — [Commission OSS Guide (July 2026), Background point 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Since 1 Jan 2015 the place of taxation is the same for B2B and B2C TBE services, but the supplier must still establish customer status because it decides who accounts for the VAT. — [TBE Explanatory Notes section 5.2](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- B2B general rule (Art. 44 Directive): services to a taxable person acting as such are supplied where that person has established his business; if supplied to a fixed establishment elsewhere, there; absent either, where he has permanent address/usually resides. — [legislation.gov.uk Directive 2006/112 Art. 44](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/44)
- Status of customer (Art. 18 Reg. 282/2011): supplier may treat an EU customer as a taxable person when it communicates a VAT ID that was successfully verified in VIES and there is no contrary information; for TBE services the supplier MAY (optional) treat any customer who has not communicated a VAT ID as a non-taxable person; the option lapses once the customer communicates a VAT ID, and the supplier must then correct invoice/VAT/OSS return. — [TBE Explanatory Notes sections 5.4, 5.5.1-5.5.8](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- If the supplier has no VAT ID but treats the customer as a taxable person, it must be able to substantiate taxable-person status, otherwise it may be liable for the VAT. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 5.5.4](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Customers established outside the EU: the supply falls outside EU VAT irrespective of customer status (unless the "effective use and enjoyment" rule of Art. 59a is applied by a Member State); supplier only needs to establish where the customer belongs. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 5.5.2](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Status can only be assessed at the time of the chargeable event (Art. 25 Reg. 282/2011); a customer that later provides its VAT ID reverses the treatment; a taxable person wrongly charged VAT cannot recover it via normal refund procedures but must go to the supplier. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 5.5.5, 5.5.7](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Any person independently carrying out an economic activity is a "taxable person" regardless of VAT registration (footnote 11 in the Notes; Art. 9(1) Directive). — [TBE Explanatory Notes fn. 11](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf); [Directive Art. 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/9)
- Practical processor behaviour: Stripe applies reverse charge only if the customer supplies an EU VAT number; a domestic tax ID that is not an EU VAT number makes the sale B2C; Stripe "assumes that all services sold to customers with a business tax ID are eligible for reverse charge" (does not check national conditions). — [Stripe Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union)

### Inferences
- Pre-recorded, automated lifetime video access = ESS (Annex I point 4 / point 5). If the product is bundled with live teacher interaction (coaching, live Q&A, graded feedback by a person), that component is excluded from ESS (Art. 7(3)(j)) and may follow other place-of-supply rules (not researched), so the bundle should be split or analysed separately.
- For Scenario A and B2B EU customers the supplier does not charge VAT; the customer self-accounts (Art. 196 is referred to in TBE Note 5.5.3). A well-run B2B flow = collect VAT ID at checkout, validate in VIES, store the validation result (date/time, ID, consultation number) with the order, issue a no-VAT "reverse charge" invoice. Without a validated ID, safest is to treat the buyer as a consumer and charge VAT (then refund/correct if the ID arrives).
- The "lifetime"/one-off nature does not change classification (still a supply of ESS); VAT is declared in the period of the chargeable event (timing rules clarified from 1 Jan 2027, see Q9 — not analysed for lifetime access).

### Gaps
- Primary EUR-Lex text of Directive Art. 44/58/59c and Reg. 282/2011 Arts. 7, 18, 24a-24f in the current consolidated version could not be fetched (EUR-Lex returned empty); used legislation.gov.uk snapshot (31 Dec 2020) and Commission notes dated April 2014 (TBE Notes) instead. Later amendments to Annex I wording were not verified.
- Art. 18 Reg. 282/2011 text itself (as opposed to the Commission's commentary) was not read.
- Treatment of bundles (video + live component) and of "membership"/continuous elements not researched.
- Art. 59a "effective use and enjoyment" implementation per Member State not researched.

## 2. Non-EU seller without EU establishment: non-Union OSS (registration, returns, payment, currency, records, invoices, input VAT)

### Takeaway
There is no registration threshold: the first euro of B2C ESS to an EU consumer is taxable in the consumer's Member State. The seller registers once in a Member State of its free choice (non-Union OSS), files a nil-or-more quarterly return by the end of the month after quarter, pays in EUR by the same date, keeps records 10 years, has no obligation to issue invoices, and cannot offset input VAT through OSS.

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility: a business that "has not established its business (place of business) in the EU, nor has a fixed establishment there"; being identified or required to be identified for VAT in the EU does not prevent use of the non-Union scheme. — [Commission OSS overview](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en); [OSS Guide (July 2026), Background point 1](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Scope: B2C services to non-taxable persons taking place in the EU (all Member States, including the Member State of identification); the scheme is optional but if chosen applies to all in-scope supplies in all Member States. — [OSS Guide (July 2026), Background and Part 2 q.1](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Worked EC example: a US trader registers in Ireland for the non-Union scheme and declares electronic services to non-taxable persons in Finland, Sweden and Ireland, all in the non-Union return including the Irish ones. — [OSS Guide (July 2026), Part 2 q.1](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Free choice of Member State of identification (MSI); MSI allocates a VAT ID in format EUxxxyyyyyz usable only for non-Union supplies; MSI may refuse registration (e.g. quarantine, already registered); MSI can be changed at any time with effect at end of calendar quarter. — [Commission "Register to OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en); [OSS Guide, Part 1a q.13](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Registration takes effect on the first day of the quarter after notification; if supplies begin earlier, the scheme can apply from the first supply provided the MSI is informed by the 10th day of the month following that first supply, otherwise the seller must register and account in each Member State of consumption directly. Changes to registration data must be notified by the 10th of the following month. — [OSS Guide, Part 1a q.6 and q.8](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Returns: quarterly, electronic, due by the end of the month following the quarter (Q1 30 Apr; Q2 31 Jul; Q3 31 Oct; Q4 31 Jan); nil return required for quarters without supplies; cannot be submitted before the period ends; deadline not moved for weekends/holidays. — [Commission "Declare and pay in OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en); [OSS Guide, Part 2 q.2 and q.10](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Return content: per Member State of consumption, taxable amount and VAT at standard and reduced rate; rates from TEDB; corrections go into a later return (within 3 years); supplies exempt in the consumption state are not included. — [OSS Guide, Part 2 q.4, q.7, q.11](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Currency: one currency, generally euro (an MSI outside the euro area may require its national currency); supplies in other currencies converted at the ECB rate published on the last day of the tax period. — [OSS Guide, Part 2 q.15](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf); [Commission "Declare and pay in OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en)
- Payment: to the MSI quoting the return's unique reference number, by the return deadline; counts as paid only when it reaches the MSI bank account; no payment plans via OSS. — [Commission "Declare and pay in OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en)
- Late filing: MSI sends reminder on day 10; penalties are set by the Member State of consumption; reminders for three consecutive periods unanswered within 10 days = persistent non-compliance = exclusion + 2-year quarantine from all OSS schemes; same for payment (not applied if outstanding amount < EUR 100 per return) and for records not produced within one month. — [Commission "Leaving OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/deregistration-oss-exclusion_en); [OSS Guide, Part 2 q.3](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Voluntary exit: notify MSI at least 15 days before end of the preceding quarter (e.g. by 15 June to leave from 1 July); exclusion also if no supplies for 8 consecutive quarters or the conditions are no longer met "for example by moving their business to an EU Member State or setting up a fixed establishment there". — [Commission "Leaving OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/deregistration-oss-exclusion_en)
- Records: kept 10 years from the end of the year of the transaction (even after leaving the scheme); contents per Art. 63c Reg. 282/2011 incl. Member State of consumption, type, date, VAT payable, payments on account, and "information used to determine the place where the customer is established"; made available electronically on request. — [Commission "Record keeping and audits in OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/record-keeping-and-audits-oss_en); [OSS Guide, Part 4](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Invoices: non-Union scheme has "no obligation for the supplier to issue an invoice"; if the supplier chooses to invoice, the rules of the Member State of identification apply. — [OSS Guide, Part 4 Table 7](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf); [Commission record-keeping page](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/record-keeping-and-audits-oss_en); legal basis Art. 219a(2)(b) Directive in [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/219a)
- Input VAT: VAT on business expenses incurred in a Member State of consumption cannot be offset in the OSS return; it must be claimed via the electronic refund procedure (Directive 2008/9/EC), the 13th VAT Directive procedure (86/560/EEC) for non-EU businesses, or the domestic return if registered there. From 1 Jan 2027 Member States may require a non-EU trader using the non-Union scheme to appoint a tax representative for 13th Directive refund requests. — [OSS Guide, Part 2 q.9 (Topic 5)](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf); [Directive Art. 368](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/368) (non-established taxable persons may not deduct under Art. 168; refund per Directive 86/560/EEC)
- No tax representative is needed to use the non-Union OSS (Stripe's statement; the EC guide lists none for this scheme, only for the import scheme). — [Stripe Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union); [OSS Guide, Background point 3](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Threshold: the EUR 10,000 B2C TBE threshold requires the supplier to be established (or have permanent address/usual residence) in only one Member State; suppliers established outside the EU with EU fixed establishments cannot use it; once exceeded the general rule applies without exception. — [OSS Guide, Background point 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf); [Commission OSS overview](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en)
- Processor tooling: Stripe Tax offers automated filing of non-Union OSS returns in Ireland for eligible businesses (and partners such as Taxually, Marosa, HOST). — [Stripe Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union)

### Inferences
- A non-EU seller with no EU establishment does not qualify for the EUR 10,000 threshold (it is not "established in only one Member State"), which is consistent with the "no threshold" premise: VAT is due from the first sale. (The guides state the threshold conditions; they do not expressly say "a seller with zero EU establishments can't use it" - this is derived.)
- The non-Union VAT ID (EU…) is only usable for OSS returns; it is not a domestic VAT number and (inference) should not be shown on B2B reverse-charge invoices as a VAT ID for VIES purposes.
- Choice of MSI is a practical choice: Ireland is the example used by the Commission and Stripe automates Irish filing; Poland is also available. Choosing Poland as MSI while the owner is actually managing from Poland would be inconsistent with the eligibility premise (see Q3).
- Practical VAT mechanics: price is gross-inclusive or VAT-on-top per consumer country; VAT rate = rate of consumer's Member State on the date of supply (TEDB).

### Gaps
- Chargeable-event timing for lifetime/one-off purchases not analysed; the July 2026 guide's "Topic 3" (Arts. 66, 167a, 226 pt 7a) explains ViDA clarifications effective 1 Jan 2027 but I did not read it in full.
- Whether any Member State of identification imposes extra requirements on a non-EU single-member LLC (e.g. proof of incorporation, apostille, local-language forms) was not researched except for Poland (see Q4).
- Penalty regimes of individual Member States of consumption for late OSS returns not researched.
- Details of Art. 58/59c and Art. 359 in the current consolidated Directive not verified from EUR-Lex (legislation.gov.uk copy of Art. 359 predates the July 2021 reform and describes only telecom/broadcasting/ESS; the Commission's 2026 guide describes the scheme as covering B2C services generally).

## 3. Where is the LLC "established" for VAT if the sole owner manages it from Poland? (Art. 44 Directive, Art. 10-11 Reg. 282/2011, CJEU)

### Takeaway
For VAT, a business is "established" where the functions of its central administration are carried out, with the place where essential decisions on general management are taken prevailing when other indicators do not settle it; a mere postal/registered address is not enough. A one-person LLC whose owner takes all management decisions from Poland has a strong risk of being treated as established in Poland, which would take it out of the non-Union OSS. No direct VAT ruling on a Wyoming LLC run from Poland was found. **Flag: requires a Polish tax adviser and, ideally, a binding individual interpretation (interpretacja indywidualna) from KIS.**

### Cited Findings
- Art. 10(1) Reg. 282/2011: for Arts. 44 and 45 the place where the business is established is "the place where the functions of the business's central administration are carried out". Art. 10(2): account is taken of the place where essential decisions concerning general management are taken, where the registered office is located and where management meets; if these do not allow determination with certainty, the place of essential decisions "shall take precedence". Art. 10(3): "The mere presence of a postal address may not be taken to be the place of establishment". — [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Art. 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/10)
- Art. 11 Reg. 282/2011: a fixed establishment requires "a sufficient degree of permanence and a suitable structure in terms of human and technical resources"; a VAT identification number alone is not sufficient. — [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Art. 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/11)
- Art. 44 Directive: B2B place of supply is where the recipient "has established his business"; fixed establishment of the recipient only if the services are provided to it. — [legislation.gov.uk Directive Art. 44](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/44)
- Commission OSS guide: "the Member State of establishment" and "fixed establishment" concepts as above; the non-Union scheme applies only to a taxable person that has neither a place of business nor a fixed establishment in the EU. — [OSS Guide (July 2026), Background points 1, 7](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- CJEU Planzer Luxembourg (C-73/06, 28 June 2007; reported by secondary sources): the "seat of economic activity" is the place where essential decisions concerning the general management of the company are taken and where its central administrative functions are carried out. — secondary summaries: [OJ summary reproduced on prawo.pl (C 2007/199)](https://www.prawo.pl/akty/dz-u-ue-c-2007-199-10,67704141.html) (page returned 404 on direct fetch; content seen via search snippet); [Dutch commentary on place of establishment (inview.nl)](https://www.inview.nl/document/id5c703566249baed8a4a51b116a3d4c4a)
- CJEU Welmory (C-605/12, 16 Oct 2014; via secondary briefing): the recipient's place of business is the primary reference point; a fixed establishment is considered only where that "does not lead to a rational result or creates a conflict with another Member State" (para 53); an FE needs sufficient permanence and a suitable structure of human and technical resources (para 58); national court determines the facts (para 62). Welmory was a Polish case (Cypriot Ltd using Polish company's infrastructure). — [VATupdate briefing of Welmory C-605/12](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/07/29/briefing-document-podcast-ecj-vat-c-605-12-welmory-clarification-of-fixed-establishment-concept-in-vat/)
- Polish commentary (Rzeczpospolita, tax adviser T. Krywan, 2 June 2016): for a US LLC run by a Polish resident selling electronic codes, the VAT business seat is the place of central management (Art. 10 Reg. 282/2011) and "because the owner runs the LLC from Poland ... its seat is in Poland", so B2C sales are taxable in Poland at 23% unless the small-business exemption applies. The article cites no CJEU case law and no official interpretation. — [pro.rp.pl article (2016)](https://pro.rp.pl/podatki/art3633911-handel-w-internecie-a-vat-miejsce-wykonywania-uslug-decyduje-o-opodatkowaniu)
- Commercial blog by a Polish radca prawny (2015-2025 comments): an LLC managed from Poland should be registered as a Polish VAT taxpayer; EU case law and Polish courts look at actual management place, not statutory seat; reports (2017) of Polish tax offices refusing VAT registration and treating services as imports (unconfirmed by the author). Low reliability. — [rejestracjaspolkiwdelaware.pl](https://rejestracjaspolkiwdelaware.pl/spolka-llc-jako-podatnik-podatku-vat/)
- KIS practice found on the related fixed-establishment question: a company whose decisions are taken abroad and with only a phone/computer and one director in Poland was held not to have a Polish fixed establishment; interpretations of 2025-2026 stress human and technical resources. These concern FE (not the principal seat) and other facts. — [search result list: inforlex KIS interpretations](https://www.inforlex.pl/dok/tresc,FOB0000000000006983637,Interpretacja-indywidualna-z-dnia-16-czerwca-2025-r-Dyrektor-Krajowej-Informacji-Skarbowej-sygn-0114-KDIP1-2-4012-246-2025-1-JO.html)
- A 2023 KIS interpretation (0113-KDIPT2-3.4011.348.2023.2.KKA) treated an LLC's activity as "entirely conducted and managed from Poland" - but it is a PIT (income tax) ruling and outside scope; mentioned only as an indicator of how Polish authorities describe such structures. — [interpretacje.pl](https://www.interpretacje.pl/pit/9277909,skutki-podatkowe-uczestnictwa-w-spolce-llc-w-stanach-zjednoczonych.html)

### Inferences
- Under Art. 10(2), if the owner (sole member and sole manager) takes all essential decisions while resident in and working from Poland, "place of essential decisions" = Poland; the registered agent address in Wyoming is, at most, the "registered office" criterion, and Art. 10(3) denies weight to a mere postal address. The tie-break therefore points to Poland.
- A company with no employees and no premises is typically hard to place in a *fixed* establishment sense (Art. 11), but that is the wrong question: the issue is where the *business itself* (the principal seat) is established, not whether there is a branch. Absence of staff does not help here; it makes the owner's location the only management location.
- Facts that would matter: where contracts are signed and decisions taken, where the owner physically works, whether anyone else (US-based manager/officers) takes real decisions, where books and bank accounts are controlled. Moving to a truly foreign management (not merely nominal) would be required to sustain Scenario A. This is a factual determination and tax authorities can look through formal structure.
- For VAT the LLC (a legal person carrying out the activity independently) is the taxable person; US "check-the-box"/income-tax transparency does not change this (inference from Art. 9(1); blog source claims the same).
- Scenario B is the more probable legal outcome for a person who really runs a one-person LLC from his Polish home, though no authoritative ruling directly on point was found; therefore treat Scenario A as at-risk until a Polish adviser confirms.

### Gaps
- No KIS interpretation, NSA/WSA judgment or CJEU case dealing specifically with a non-EU single-member LLC run by a Polish-resident sole owner for VAT purposes was found (searches in Polish and English; sip.mf.gov.pl database itself not searchable via the tool).

## 4. If the LLC is established in Poland (Scenario B): consequences and how it differs from Scenario A

### Takeaway
Non-Union OSS is closed; the LLC becomes a Polish-established taxable person: Polish VAT registration (VAT-R) as active VAT payer (unless the PLN 240,000 small-business exemption is used and applicable), Polish consumers at 23% in the Polish return, other-EU consumers either at Polish VAT up to the EUR 10,000 threshold or via Union OSS at destination rates, B2B EU services reported in VAT-UE, and B2B invoices in KSeF. Penalty-free KSeF period ends 31 Dec 2026 (penalty start possibly deferred to 2028 - only a proposal).

### Cited Findings
- Union scheme is for taxable persons "established in the EU or with a fixed establishment there"; its Member State of identification is the Member State where the business is established; it uses the existing national VAT ID (must be obtained first); services supplied in the Member State of establishment are NOT declared in OSS but in the domestic return; only B2C supplies in other Member States are declared in the Union OSS. — [OSS Guide (July 2026), Background points 1, 6, Part 1a, Part 2 q.1](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf); [Commission OSS overview](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en)
- EUR 10,000 threshold (Art. 59c Directive): applies if the supplier is established in only one Member State, supplies B2C TBE services to customers in other Member States, and total (ex VAT, summed with distance sales of goods) does not exceed EUR 10,000 in the current AND preceding calendar year; then the supplier's own Member State rules apply and OSS is not relevant; supplier may opt out (bound 2 calendar years); Union OSS registration automatically means destination rules; once exceeded the general rule applies "without exception". — [OSS Guide (July 2026), Background point 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf); [Commission OSS overview](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en)
- Polish implementation: Art. 28k VAT Act (B2C TBE place of supply = where customer is established/resides); threshold PLN 42,000 (about EUR 10,000) per Ministry of Finance material from 2019 (the PLN equivalent is converted and may differ in 2026); EU-registration form VIU-R, quarterly return VIU-DO, deadline end of month after quarter, zero return if no sales; competent office: Drugi Urząd Skarbowy Warszawa-Śródmieście. — secondary: [poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl on VAT OSS](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-vat-oss-zasady-rozliczania-podatku-vat-w-ramach-pakietu-e-commerce) and search-derived summaries of [podatki.gov.pl VIU-DO instructions](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/7274/instrukcja-wypelniania-viu-do.pdf), [ifirma on EE marker](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/oznaczenie-ee-kiedy-nalezy-zastosowac/)
- Polish B2B treatment (art. 28b): ESS to a foreign business customer is not taxed in Poland; invoice with "NP" and "Odwrotne obciążenie"; reported in JPK_V7 fields P_11/P_12 and, for EU business customers, in the monthly VAT-UE summary (art. 100 ust. 1 pkt 4), which requires registration as an EU VAT payer (VAT-UE). — [ifirma.pl, ESS to businesses (Dec 2020)](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/uslugi-elektroniczne-na-rzecz-firm)
- EE marker in JPK: telecom/broadcast/electronic services to non-taxable persons (art. 28k) are flagged "EE" (source dated Oct 2020). — [ifirma.pl, "Oznaczenie EE"](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/oznaczenie-ee-kiedy-nalezy-zastosowac/)
- Polish small-business exemption: for 2026 the limit is PLN 240,000 of previous-year sales (200,000 in 2025), pro rata for mid-year start; exclusions list in art. 113 ust. 13 does not mention electronic services; sales taxed abroad under OSS are not counted (art. 113 ust. 2 covers distance sales of goods). — [poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl (page dated 11 June 2026)](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-zwolnienie-podmiotowe-z-vat-czy-cos-sie-zmienilo)
- Since 1 Jan 2025 the exemption is for a taxpayer with the seat of business in Poland (search-result summary); a commenter on the blog argues third-country entities cannot use it - unresolved. — [search-derived summary of Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1721](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2024/1721/ogl/pol/pdf); [rejestracjaspolkiwdelaware.pl](https://rejestracjaspolkiwdelaware.pl/spolka-llc-jako-podatnik-podatku-vat/)
- Even if exempt, a taxpayer who supplies services to EU business customers (art. 28b) generally still registers as "VAT-UE" and files VAT-UE (not verified here; flagged as to-check). — see [ifirma.pl, ESS to businesses](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/uslugi-elektroniczne-na-rzecz-firm) (VAT-UE requirement)
- KSeF (Krajowy System e-Faktur): mandatory issuing of structured invoices from 1 Feb 2026 for firms with 2024 sales above PLN 200m (gross) and from 1 Apr 2026 for all others; until 31 Dec 2026 invoices outside KSeF are allowed if monthly invoiced sales (gross) do not exceed PLN 10,000; B2C invoices through KSeF are voluntary. — [KSeF portal, "Od kiedy trzeba wystawiać faktury w KSeF"](http://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/od-kiedy-trzeba-wystawiac-faktury-w-ksef/); [KSeF portal, consumers](http://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/konsumenci-i-osoby-fizyczne/)
- KSeF penalties: Head of KAS (12 Jan 2026) said no penalties in 2026 for not joining or for errors; art. 106ni penalties are due from 1 Jan 2027; on 16 Sept 2026 the Ministry of Finance said it wants to defer them to 2028 (draft amendment work only begun - not law). — [infor.pl report](https://ksiegowosc.infor.pl/ksef/7497035,szef-kas-do-konca-2026-roku-nie-bedziemy-karac-za-nieprzystapienie-do-ksef-i-popelniane-bledy-w-tym-systemie-wiemy-jakie-kary-beda-stosowane-od-2027-r.html); [jpk.info.pl, 18 Sept 2026](https://jpk.info.pl/aktualnosci/2026/kary-za-omijanie-ksef-dopiero-w-2028/)
- KSeF and foreign entities: the obligation does not apply to a taxpayer with neither a seat nor a fixed establishment in Poland (it may use KSeF voluntarily if registered as active Polish VAT payer; KIS interpretations of 13 Dec 2024 and 27 May 2025 cited). — [poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl (5 Dec 2025)](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-ksef-dla-podmiotow-zagranicznych-czy-jest-obowiazkowy)
- A taxpayer with its seat in Poland must issue KSeF invoices even for sales whose place of supply is abroad (B2B) - KIS interpretation of 11 Feb 2026, 0113-KDIPT1-2.4012.1035.2025.2.AJB (Art. 106a pkt 2, Art. 106ga). — [inforlex.pl](https://www.inforlex.pl/dok/tresc,FOB0000000000007524083,Interpretacja-indywidualna-z-dnia-11-lutego-2026-r-Dyrektor-Krajowej-Informacji-Skarbowej-sygn-0113-KDIPT1-2-4012-1035-2025-2-AJB.html)
- Polish non-Union OSS (Scenario A): registration form VIN-R filed electronically only via e-Deklaracje with a qualified electronic signature to Naczelnik Drugiego Urzędu Skarbowego Warszawa-Śródmieście; payments in EUR (IBAN required; EUR account recommended by the practitioner source); returns by end of month after quarter. — [ifirma.pl on VIN-R](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/vin-r-rejestracja-do-oss-w-ramach-procedury-nieunijnej/); [podatki.gov.pl VIN-R instruction](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/6922/instrukcja-wypełniania-vin-r-okres-przejściowy.pdf)
- A 'domestic' Polish article claims a non-EU seller must register for Polish VAT for Polish consumers — contradicted by the Commission guide stating that the non-Union OSS also covers supplies taking place in the MSI (Irish example). — [poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl (search summary, unverified)](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-nieunijna-procedura-vat-oss-w-pakiecie-vat-e-commerce); contradicted by [OSS Guide Part 2 q.1](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- The Commission 2-evidence simplification: where a supplier established in a Member State has supplies of TBE to non-taxable persons not exceeding EUR 100,000 (current and preceding calendar year) it may rely on one item of evidence from a person involved in the supply other than the supplier or customer (Art. 24b second para.); the provision is worded "provided by a taxable person from his business establishment or a fixed establishment located in a Member State". — [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Art. 24b](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/24b)

### Inferences
Comparison (derived from the findings above):

| Item | Scenario A: no EU establishment | Scenario B: established in Poland |
|---|---|---|
| OSS scheme | Non-Union OSS, any MSI of choice, EU-prefixed VAT ID | Union OSS, MSI = Poland, uses Polish NIP/VAT ID (VIU-R, VIU-DO) |
| Registration | Only OSS (VIN-R if Poland as MSI); no domestic registration | Polish VAT registration (VAT-R) as VAT payer (or exempt, up to PLN 240k/yr; still VAT-UE for EU B2B) + VIU-R when going above EUR 10k/choosing OSS |
| B2C consumers in the home state | Declared in the non-Union OSS return (any MSI incl. Poland); rate of consumer's state | Polish consumers: domestic return at 23% (JPK_V7), NOT in OSS |
| B2C other EU states | From first euro at destination rate via non-Union OSS | Up to EUR 10,000/year at Polish 23%; above: destination rates via Union OSS (or direct registrations) |
| Threshold | None (not established in one MS) | EUR 10,000 (PLN 42,000 reference) cross-border B2C; domestic sales unaffected |
| B2B EU customers | No VAT, customer reverse-charges; no VAT-UE; no EU registration | No Polish VAT, "NP / odwrotne obciążenie", VAT-UE, JPK_V7 reporting, KSeF invoice |
| Invoices | Not required for B2C; MSI rules if issued | Polish invoice rules; B2B via KSeF (from 1 Apr 2026 for most firms); B2C voluntary |
| Evidence for customer location | Two non-contradictory items (no 100k simplification) | Two items; one item sufficient up to EUR 100,000 if third-party evidence (Art. 24b 2nd para) |
| Input VAT | Not via OSS; 13th Directive refund (non-EU) | Domestic return deduction in Poland (Polish rules); EU refunds under Directive 2008/9 |
| Reporting | Quarterly OSS return | Monthly/quarterly JPK_V7 + VAT-UE + quarterly OSS return (VIU-DO) |

- Using the non-Union OSS while truly established in Poland is a mis-registration risk: the scheme's eligibility premise fails, exclusion is possible, and Polish consumer sales could be assessed at 23% with interest/penalties. The magnitude cannot be quantified from the sources found.
- Under Polish rules the small-business exemption (PLN 240k) and the EUR 10k threshold could make Scenario B light-touch at small volumes (no VAT charged in Poland while below the exemption), but the exemption is not a registration-free regime for EU B2B/OSS flows; verify with an adviser.
- Mix: if seat is in Poland, B2C sales to non-EU consumers (e.g. US, UK) are outside EU VAT; Polish VAT-registered sellers report them under the "outside the territory" fields in JPK_V7 (not verified).

### Gaps
- JPK_V7 structure after KSeF (2026 versions) and exact EE/OSS field handling not verified (sources date from 2020).
- Whether VAT-R/VAT-UE registration is required for an exempt (art. 113) taxpayer supplying ESS to EU business customers: only inferred from general Polish practice; not confirmed with a primary source.
- Whether a third-country-incorporated entity may use art. 113 exemption in Poland - disputed in secondary sources, no authoritative answer found.
- Polish tax-representative requirement for non-EU entities registering domestically (if not using OSS) not researched.

## 6. Evidence rules for the customer's location (Art. 24a/24b/24f) and payment-processor practice

### Takeaway
For ESS the supplier must determine the consumer's location from two items of non-contradictory evidence (Art. 24b(d)), chosen from the non-exhaustive Art. 24f list (billing address, IP/geolocation, bank details, SIM country code, fixed-line location, other commercially relevant info). Items must be different (not duplicates), and the supplier remains responsible even if a payment processor does the verification.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 24b(d): where no specific presumption applies, the customer is presumed to be located where identified by the supplier on the basis of two items of non-contradictory evidence "as listed in Article 24f"; Art. 24f list: (a) billing address, (b) IP address or any geolocation method, (c) bank details (location of the account or billing address held by the bank), (d) SIM-card country code, (e) location of fixed land line, (f) other commercially relevant information. — [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Art. 24b](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/24b); [Art. 24f](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/24f)
- Three items of non-contradictory evidence are only needed to rebut a specific presumption (Art. 24a, 24b(a)-(c)); the general presumption needs two. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 9.5.5](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Items must be different and not duplicate each other: a billing address later confirmed by self-certification = one item; bank details confirmed by the payment provider = one item; IP address and geolocation to the same place = one item. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 9.5.5](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- An electronic address to which an e-invoice is sent is not a "billing address"; IP address is a valid item but not a billing address; a PO box is not sufficient for a consumer. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 9.5.2](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- "Other commercially relevant information" examples: unique country payment methods, consumer trading history (historical IP, billing address), documentation from payment service providers (where shared with the supplier), customer self-certification (country, bank/card information). — [TBE Explanatory Notes 9.5.1](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Verification: supplier bases itself on information from the customer and verifies with "normal commercial security measures" (identity/payment checks); not per-transaction after initial verification, but periodic proactive verification; conflict between evidence sets: supplier decides which are most reliable, priority to the place that best ensures taxation at actual consumption; with a universal price the billing address is a quite reliable indicator. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 9.5.4, 9.5.6](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Reliance on third parties: correct determination "remains with the supplier"; verification by a payment provider does not relieve the supplier of responsibility in case of misuse or abuse. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 9.5.9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Tax authority may rebut only where there are indications of misuse/abuse by the supplier; rebuttal by the customer is not possible. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 8.4.1-8.4.2](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Records must include the information used to determine the customer location. — [Commission "Record keeping and audits in OSS"](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/record-keeping-and-audits-oss_en)
- Simplification: one item of evidence from a third party involved in the supply (not supplier/customer) is enough where supplier's cross-border TBE supplies do not exceed EUR 100,000 in current and preceding year, but only for supplies "from his business establishment or a fixed establishment located in a Member State". — [legislation.gov.uk Reg. 282/2011 Art. 24b](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/24b)
- Stripe Tax practice: uses a single address as the location (Checkout: address collected in session; otherwise shipping address, billing address, then payment-method billing details, then IP address) and does not compare two pieces of evidence; Stripe stores and retains all location evidence on the Customer object; it can fall back to card-issuer country + postal code; for EU services "an IP address is more likely to resolve to an accurate address". — [Stripe, customer locations](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/customer-locations); [Stripe Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union)
- Merchant-of-record / platform route: Art. 9a Reg. 282/2011 presumes that a taxable person "taking part" in the supply of ESS through a portal/marketplace acts in its own name (deemed supplier); businesses that only process payments are expressly not caught. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 3.4.2-3.4.3](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf); [Stripe, marketplace tax liability](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union)

### Inferences
- Stripe's single-address approach does not itself guarantee Art. 24b(d) compliance; the seller should additionally retain the two-item set (e.g., billing address collected at checkout + IP geolocation or card-issuer country) and keep both in the OSS records for 10 years. Card BIN/issuing country counts as "bank details" evidence (Art. 24f(c)) (inference; the notes do not name BIN explicitly).
- Using a true merchant-of-record (e.g. a reseller platform that is the contractual seller) would shift VAT liability to the intermediary (Art. 9a Reg. 282/2011 presumption); a plain payment processor (Stripe as PSP) does not.
- The EUR 100k single-evidence simplification is unavailable in Scenario A (not established in a Member State) but available in Scenario B.

### Gaps
- Art. 24a (physical locations) not relevant for online video; not analysed.
- Current wording of Art. 24b/24f in the consolidated text (after the 2019 amendment) read via the 2020 UK copy; no later amendments known but not verified.
- Practices of other processors (PayPal, Adyen, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy as MoR) not researched.

## 7. Invoicing for B2C ESS under OSS; VAT ID validation

### Takeaway
Under OSS no invoice is required for B2C ESS; if one is issued, the rules of the Member State of identification apply. For B2B EU customers an invoice without VAT stating reverse charge is needed, based on a VIES-validated VAT ID. Under Scenario B Polish invoicing and KSeF rules apply to B2B invoices.

### Cited Findings
- Non-Union scheme: no general obligation to invoice; MSI rules apply if invoice issued; same for Union scheme (no obligation; MSI rules). — [OSS Guide, Part 4, Table 7](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- Legal basis: Art. 219a Directive - where the supplier uses a special scheme of Chapter 6 of Title XII, the invoicing rules of the Member State of identification apply. — [legislation.gov.uk Directive Art. 219a](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/219a)
- Customer VAT-ID validation: supplier may regard an EU customer as a taxable person when it communicates a VAT ID verified in VIES and nothing suggests otherwise; for TBE the supplier may treat customers without a VAT ID as consumers. — [TBE Explanatory Notes 5.4, 5.5.8](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Reverse-charge invoice must state the reverse charge instead of a VAT amount; some Member States require the customer VAT ID on the invoice for cross-border no-VAT treatment. — [Stripe Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union); [TBE Explanatory Notes 5.5.9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Polish side for Scenario B: B2B invoice to foreign business "NP" + "Odwrotne obciążenie"; KSeF mandatory from 1 Apr 2026 for most taxpayers with Polish seat even if place of supply is abroad; B2C KSeF invoices voluntary. — [ifirma.pl](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/uslugi-elektroniczne-na-rzecz-firm); [KIS interpretation 11 Feb 2026](https://www.inforlex.pl/dok/tresc,FOB0000000000007524083,Interpretacja-indywidualna-z-dnia-11-lutego-2026-r-Dyrektor-Krajowej-Informacji-Skarbowej-sygn-0113-KDIPT1-2-4012-1035-2025-2-AJB.html); [KSeF portal](http://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/konsumenci-i-osoby-fizyczne/)

### Inferences
- Even though invoices are not required for B2C OSS sales, consumer "receipts" are practical (refund handling, customer support) and Poland (as MSI) has its own formal invoice requirements if one is issued; if the MSI is another state (e.g. Ireland), that state's rules govern.
- In Scenario A a "reverse charge" B2B invoice is issued by a non-EU supplier with no EU VAT ID: there is no EU VAT number of its own to show (the OSS EU-number is for OSS only); the invoice would show customer VAT ID and a reverse-charge note. Not verified against any Member State's invoice-content rules.

### Gaps
- Minimum content of invoices in the MSI of choice (Ireland, Poland etc.) for B2C and B2B not researched.
- Art. 18/19 Reg. 282/2011 consolidated text and VIES evidence retention rules (consultation number) not researched from primary sources.
- Whether consumers in some Member States have a right to a VAT invoice on request when the supplier uses OSS: not researched.

## 8. ViDA (VAT in the Digital Age): what matters to this seller 2026-2030

### Takeaway
ViDA (Directive 2025/516, Regulation 2025/517, Implementing Reg. 2025/518; entered into force 14 April 2025) mostly leaves this seller's B2C ESS regime unchanged. Relevant: small clarifications effective 1 Jan 2027 (OSS guide revised July 2026; non-Union OSS formally covers B2C services to EU-established and non-established customers; website data no longer a registration requirement; tax-representative possibility for refund requests), single VAT registration/OSS extension from 1 July 2028, and digital reporting/e-invoicing for intra-EU B2B transactions from 1 July 2030.

### Cited Findings
- Adoption and timing: adopted 11 March 2025, published OJ 25 March 2025; entry into force 14 April 2025; 1 Jan 2027 minor clarifications affecting OSS/IOSS users; 1 July 2028 platform deemed-supplier rules (accommodation/passenger transport; Member States may delay to 1 Jan 2030) and single VAT registration elements including the OSS extension and mandatory reverse charge for non-identified suppliers; 1 July 2030 digital reporting requirements (impact mainly cross-border B2B); 1 Jan 2035 alignment of domestic systems. — [Commission ViDA page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-digital-age-vida_en)
- The Commission revised OSS Guide (July 2026) lists the 1 Jan 2027 topics: threshold calculation clarifications (Topic 2, Art. 59c), timing of chargeable event for supplies under Union and non-Union scheme (Topic 3, Arts. 66, 167a, 226 pt 7a), non-Union scheme covering B2C services to EU-established and non-established customers (Topic 4, Art. 359), refund-procedure references (Topic 5, Arts. 368, 369j, 369w), update of registration data (Topic 9: website data no longer mandatory for non-Union OSS registration from 1 Jan 2027). A completely new version will follow for the 1 July 2028 extension. — [OSS Guide (July 2026), General information and Part 1a q.11](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- The Commission published the revised Explanatory Notes and OSS Guidelines (24 July 2026), "in force from 1 January 2027", and an addendum of 6 October 2026 (EUR 3 customs duty/EUR 2 handling fee: goods, not relevant). — [Commission OSS guides page](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/guides_en)
- From 1 Jan 2027 a Member State may require a non-EU trader using the non-Union scheme to appoint a tax representative for 13th Directive refund requests. — [OSS Guide, Part 2 q.9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/55f4ec9d-83e6-4942-9e8d-11d44c243087_en?filename=OSS%20Guidelines_revised_1Jan2027_0.pdf)
- The Commission page does not explain effects on non-EU suppliers of B2C digital services directly and refers to the legal acts. — [Commission ViDA page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-digital-age-vida_en)

### Inferences
- ViDA's platform deemed-supplier rule (2028/2030) concerns accommodation and passenger transport platforms, not video content; not relevant to this seller.
- The 2030 digital reporting/e-invoicing for intra-EU B2B supplies targets suppliers that supply goods/services across Member States; whether a non-EU supplier making reverse-charge B2B supplies falls under it (Scenario A) was not established; for Scenario B (Polish-established) it likely applies to B2B EU transactions from 1 July 2030 (inference from "mainly cross-border B2B" statement only).
- Poland's national KSeF regime (2026) is a domestic e-invoicing system independent of ViDA's 2030 deadline.

### Gaps
- Full text of Directive (EU) 2025/516 and Regulation 2025/517 not read; whether non-EU suppliers of B2B services are within the digital reporting (DRR) obligations from 1 July 2030 is unresolved.
- Content of the "single VAT registration" extension from 1 July 2028 (e.g. effect on non-EU B2C services) not researched beyond the Commission summary.
- Poland's implementation of ViDA options (e.g. mandatory e-invoicing rules for cross-border) not researched.
