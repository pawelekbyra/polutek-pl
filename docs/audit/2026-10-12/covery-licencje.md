# Licensing video covers (own performance of others' songs) for a self-hosted worldwide site, as of October 2026

Access date for all URLs: 2026-10-12. Not legal advice. "Sec." = secondary/commercial source; "Prim." = primary/official.

## 1. Which rights are needed for a cover VIDEO online (composition, master, lyrics, moral rights)

### Takeaway
A cover video needs permission for the composition (underlying song) in three layers: reproduction/mechanical, synchronisation with the picture, and public performance/making available. The master is the creator's own recording, so no master licence. Sync is never compulsory and always negotiated with the publisher/author; collecting societies usually do not give it.

### Cited Findings
- US Copyright Office: a "phonorecord" does not include sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work; the s.115 licence is for phonorecords of nondramatic musical works; licences for sound recording and composition are separate. (Prim.) — [Circular 73](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ73.pdf)
- Mechanical licence covers audio only; pairing audio with video needs a separate sync licence from the composition owner (publisher), not from an agency such as HFA. (Sec., attorney blogs) — [themusicindustrylawyer.com](https://www.themusicindustrylawyer.com/why-posting-a-cover-song-on-youtube-is-copyright-infringement/), [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/how-to-post-a-cover-song-on-youtube-legally/)
- A slideshow or single still image counts as video and needs sync. (Sec.) — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/how-to-post-a-cover-song-on-youtube-legally/)
- Streaming a video/audio on your own website adds further licences "some from the publisher, and some from organizations like ASCAP and BMI" (public performance). (Sec., 2016 attorney) — [Firemark](https://firemark.com/2016/10/20/posting-cover-videos-of-songs-entertainment-law-asked-answered/)
- Master use licence is only needed when the original recording is used; own re-recording needs none. (Sec.) — [themusicindustrylawyer.com](https://www.themusicindustrylawyer.com/why-posting-a-cover-song-on-youtube-is-copyright-infringement/)
- Poland: a new performance requires a licence on the relevant fields of exploitation; distributing an *adaptation* (opracowanie: significantly changing melody, translating lyrics) additionally requires the author's permission; the original performer's consent is not needed. Transposition, tempo, phrasing are normally not an adaptation; rearrangement for different ensemble may be. Author's integrity right and parody limits apply. (Sec., Rzeczpospolita legal column) — [rp.pl](https://www.rp.pl/opinie-prawne/art2751421-cover-a-prawo-autorskie-w-praktyce)
- US: under the compulsory licence an arrangement may not change the basic melody or fundamental character; derivative claim in the arrangement is not allowed. (Prim.) — [Circular 73](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ73.pdf), [17 USC 115](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/115)
- Publishers often ask to hear the cover before approving sync and may refuse. (Sec.) — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/how-to-post-a-cover-song-on-youtube-legally/)

### Inferences
- For a self-hosted site, rights layers are: (1) sync + reproduction (publisher or author, per song, negotiated), (2) public performance / making available online (collecting society or publisher, territory by territory), (3) lyrics display (on-screen lyrics/subtitles = part of the composition; inferred to be covered by the same publisher consent, not separately sourced), (4) author's consent if the arrangement or translated lyrics make it an "adaptation".
- Not separately sourced: lyrics-display services such as Musixmatch/LyricFind license lyrics to platforms/apps, not to individual creators' own sites (my background knowledge, unverified).

### Gaps
- No primary source found stating explicitly that a publisher's sync licence normally bundles reproduction and making-available for a website; this is common industry practice, but must be confirmed per quote.

## 2. Compulsory licence? Fair use? YouTube vs a self-hosted site

### Takeaway
No compulsory licence exists for covers in video form (s.115 is audio only); the "covers are fine" belief rests on YouTube's platform deals and Content ID tolerance, which do not exist on a self-hosted site, where the operator is the direct infringer.

### Cited Findings
- s.115 excludes sounds accompanying audiovisual works; compulsory licence cannot be used for "broadcasting, or any other public use". (Prim.) — [Circular 73](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ73.pdf)
- "Sync licenses are not compulsory"; publisher can refuse or set any price. (Sec.) — [Foximusic YouTube sync guide, updated 2 Oct 2026](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/sync-license-for-youtube-guide/)
- MLC blanket licence under s.115(d) is for qualifying digital music providers' digital phonorecord deliveries (audio). (Prim.) — [17 USC 115](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/115). (MLC's own FAQ page could not be loaded: 404.)
- YouTube: copyright owners decide whether others may reuse content; Content ID claim can block, monetise or track; YPP creators can share revenue on claimed cover videos pro rata after the publisher claims. (Prim.) — [YouTube Help 6013276](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276), [YouTube Help 3301938](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3301938)
- Content ID "doesn't read license agreements" and a claim is not a licence; most covers stay up but revenue goes to rights holder. (Sec.) — [Foximusic](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/sync-license-for-youtube-guide/)
- Risks without sync: nothing happens, DMCA takedown, cease-and-desist, lawsuit. (Sec.) — [themusicindustrylawyer.com](https://www.themusicindustrylawyer.com/why-posting-a-cover-song-on-youtube-is-copyright-infringement/)
- ZAiKS signed licence agreement with YouTube in 2011 covering music in videos in Poland, revenue from ads on partner videos. (Sec., news, dated) — [Spider's Web](https://spidersweb.pl/2011/01/zaiks-i-youtube-podpisaly-umowe-licencyjna.html)
- GEMA: platform operators need a licence which GEMA issues; GEMA handles the synchronisation right "in many instances" for such uses, members may revoke it. (Prim.) — [GEMA](https://www.gema.de/en/w/help/creators/rights/social-media/rights-creator-music-youtube-instagram-tiktok). Conflicting secondary claim that GEMA may not license sync at all (search summary of a blog, not verified); the GEMA page itself concerns platforms, not own sites.
- DMCA 512(c) safe harbour covers "storage at the direction of a user" and requires a designated agent registered with the Copyright Office. (Prim.) — [17 USC 512](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)

### Inferences
- On the creator's own site he uploads his own content, so he is the direct infringer, not a "user-directed storage" host; DMCA safe harbour and platform blanket licences do not shield him. (Legal inference; confirm with counsel.)
- Fair use: no source supports fair use for a full-length commercial-style cover video; treat "covers are fair use" as a myth (my assessment, not sourced).

### Gaps
- No source on EU DSM Directive art. 17 / DSA applicability found; art. 17 concerns online content-sharing platforms and would not apply to the operator's own uploads (background knowledge, unverified).

## 3. Services that clear covers

### Takeaway
Cheap, fast services exist for audio-only mechanical licences (Easy Song, Songfile/HFA, Limelight); none was found offering a published-price worldwide sync licence for covers on a self-hosted website. Easy Song offers "custom licensing" for video by quote; that is the nearest to a one-stop route.

### Cited Findings
- Easy Song Licensing (now easysong.com): cover licensing is a mechanical licence for audio-only physical/digital releases, $19.99 per song plus royalties to the copyright holder, 1-2 business days; "custom licensing" to "clear custom rights for video, print, and more", quote-based, price not listed; territories and platforms not stated. (Prim., site) — [easysong.com](https://www.easysong.com/)
- Older figure: $16.99/song plus royalties (Disc Makers partner page); licences "never expire". (Sec.) — [Disc Makers](https://discmakers.com/services/easy-song-licensing.asp)
- Easy Song custom licensing since 2016 covers YouTube/film/TV/advertising; partner Music Gateway describes clearance for video sync "streaming, downloadable content, film, TV..." — per quote. (Sec.) — [Music Gateway](https://musicgateway.com/blog/how-to/easy-song-partners-with-music-gateway-to-help-artists-keep-ahead-of-the-copyright-game)
- Easy Song marketing claim: any cover cleared in 1-2 days; this is for the mechanical product. (Sec.) — [Trustpilot](https://pl.trustpilot.com/review/easysong.com)
- HFA Songfile: licences for physical, permanent digital downloads, ringtones, interactive streams; up to 2,500 units / 10,000 interactive streams; processing fee about $14-16 per song plus statutory royalty; "1-2 weeks" direct HFA. (Sec., dated) — [HFA help](https://help.harryfox.com/what-is-the-songfile-streaming-license-rate), [Bandzoogle interview](https://bandzoogle.com/blog/how-to-record-and-release-cover-songs-an-interview-with-hfa)
- US statutory mechanical rate 2026: 13.1 cents per work or 2.52 cents per minute, whichever is larger. (Sec.) — [Foximusic](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/how-much-does-a-song-license-cost/)
- Limelight (Music Reports/RightsFlow): audio licences only; sync for covers is case by case through RightsFlow (2012); 2014 price about $30 for up to 25 downloads/100 streams, not covering YouTube. (Sec., old) — [Hypebot 2012](https://www.hypebot.com/hypebot/2012/01/walk-off-the-earth-cover-songs-music-videos.html), [Indie on the Move 2014](https://www.indieonthemove.com/blog/2014/1/will-i-get-sued-on-youtube-licensing-a-cover-song)
- Songfreedom is now FyrFly (fyrfly.com; site returned 403). Historic pricing from 2016 was for licensing original recordings of popular songs for video makers (single use $9.99-$49.99 per tier; commercial $99.99-$10,000+), not covers of your own performance. Current terms not verified. (Sec., dated) — [Phantom Pilots forum quoting FAQ](https://phantompilots.com/threads/licensing-popular-music-songfreedom.56511/post-546731), [SLR Lounge](https://www.slrlounge.com/finally-license-popular-music-for-25-with-songfreedom/)
- YouTube-oriented claim services for covers named in a blog: Loudr, WeAreTheHits/Songfluencer, RumbleFish/AdRev; these handle claims/monetisation on YouTube, not own-site licences. (Sec.) — [search result summary of CD Baby/Hypebot posts](https://diymusician.cdbaby.com/?p=11131)

### Inferences
- Songclearance, Loudly, Musixmatch, Songtradr, Rightsify, Soundreef, Audio Network, Epidemic: no source found showing they clear third-party covers for a self-hosted site; they are mostly libraries/lyrics/CMO services (background knowledge, unverified). Loudr: unverified current status.
- None of the services advertise a flat worldwide, perpetual, self-hosted-website sync licence for covers. Best case, Easy Song or Music Gateway acts as intermediary and gets quotes per publisher.

### Gaps

## 4. Direct licensing approach and costs

### Takeaway
Direct sync licences are negotiated per song; there is no published tariff. Indie/unsigned publishers: tens to hundreds of dollars; majors: case by case, often hundreds to thousands, weeks to months, with refusal possible; paid access raises the price and may change the licence class.

### Cited Findings
- Fee ranges (2026 guide): micro-sync $5-$500 per use; indie/unsigned artists $50-$1,000 negotiated directly; famous songs "no fixed price", thousands to six figures for sync + master; indie festival/film sync hundreds to low thousands. Negotiating with well-known artists can take "weeks or months". (Sec., vendor of royalty-free music) — [Foximusic YouTube guide](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/sync-license-for-youtube-guide/), [Foximusic cost guide](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/how-much-does-a-song-license-cost/)
- Typically a flat upfront fee rather than per view. (Sec.) — [Hypebot sync licensing](https://www.hypebot.com/sync-licensing-how-does-it-work/)
- Find the publisher via collecting-society databases (ZAiKS, ASCAP/BMI repertory, MLC public search) — [ZAiKS role per rp.pl](https://www.rp.pl/opinie-prawne/art2751421-cover-a-prawo-autorskie-w-praktyce); PRS directs sync enquiries to synclicensing@prsformusic.com. — [PRS FAQ](https://prsformusic.com/licences/using-music-online/digital-music-licence/digital-music-licence-faqs)
- Music Gateway (sync agent) process: formal request, quoted fee. (Sec., via search summary; page 403) — [Music Gateway](https://musicgateway.com/music-licensing/cover-songs)

### Inferences
- Term and territory are negotiated: ask for worldwide, perpetual (or 5 years), all online media incl. paid access, to avoid renewal and re-negotiation when the site goes paid; publishers will price that higher than free/ad-free single-territory use.
- A flat fee for a mid-size publisher plausibly $100-$1,000 per song; majors may exceed $1,000 or refuse without a track record. These are my estimates, not sourced quotes.

### Gaps
- No published minimum fees or SLA from Sony Music Publishing, Universal Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, Kobalt or BMG; no number for "own site, paid access". Needs actual quote requests.

## 5. Poland-specific (ZAiKS, STOART) and PRS/GEMA contrasts

### Takeaway
ZAiKS can license online making-available of its repertoire, with tariffs tied to revenue incl. paywall/ads, but ZAiKS licence does not replace the publisher's/author's sync consent. STOART (performers/producers) is irrelevant for his own performance. Rights are territorial, and ZAiKS tariffs seem aimed at Polish operators.

### Cited Findings
- ZAiKS tariff tables list: Table 13 public making available on demand, Table 12 live streaming, Table 11 webcasting, Table 9 reproduction on sound and audiovisual media; synchronisation is not mentioned on the rates page; online licensing handled by internet@zaiks.org.pl. (Prim.) — [ZAiKS rates](https://zaiks.org.pl/licencje/wynagrodzenia-autorskie)
- ZAiKS online licence calculator (as summarised): free making available on a website for listening, background music for the website, non-commercial webcast; not for foreign online services. (Sec. summary of primary page, unverified wording) — [ZAiKS calculator](https://online-app.zaiks.org.pl/view/calculation)
- ZAiKS tariff general provisions (grand-rights table, resolution 179/292/2023): for paid or income-generating making available, fee is a percentage of "wpływy" (receipts) with a minimum amount; "wpływy" includes access fees, sponsoring, advertising; for non-paid use fixed minimum rates apply. (Prim., PDF) — [ZAiKS tabela](https://app.zaiks.org.pl/media/us1bpsll/tabela-stawek-wynagrodzen-autorskich-za-publiczne-udostepniane-utworow.pdf). Caution: this is the "wielkie prawa" (opera/theatre/audiobook) table; the song ("małe prawa") on-demand table was not read, but the receipts structure is likely similar.
- Source describing Polish practice: ZAiKS (or STOART) licence does not suffice for synchronising music with image; authors/rights holders must be approached directly. (Sec., Chambers) — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/synchronization-license)
- ZAiKS: works where authors died more than 70 years ago need no licence; for repertoire not under ZAiKS, ask the rightsholder directly. (Sec.) — [rp.pl](https://www.rp.pl/opinie-prawne/art2751421-cover-a-prawo-autorskie-w-praktyce)
- Since 20 Sep 2024 Polish implementation of DSM/SatCab II introduced an inalienable right to remuneration for online making available (per a search summary; not verified). 
- PRS (UK): Limited Online Music Licence for own website, intended for turnover under about GBP 12,500; does NOT cover synchronisation; excludes adaptations; sync via publisher. (Prim./Sec.) — [PRS FAQ](https://prsformusic.com/licences/using-music-online/digital-music-licence/digital-music-licence-faqs), [OneDance guide](https://www.onedanceuk.org/media/oosggjbn/streaming-youth-dance-online-music-rights-and-licensing.pdf)
- GEMA: members' own-presentation licence for own works on own website; not for covers (as summarised). (Sec.) — [GEMA information_eigenpraesentation.pdf](https://www.gema.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Musikurheber/Informationen/information_eigenpraesentation.pdf) (PDF unreadable; claim from search summary).
- ZAiKS-YouTube arrangement exists only for YouTube. (Sec.) — [Spider's Web](https://spidersweb.pl/2011/01/zaiks-i-youtube-podpisaly-umowe-licencyjna.html)

### Inferences
- A Polish individual can get ZAiKS performance/making-available licence for Polish-territory use fairly easily; the hard, per-song part is sync/reproduction, which remains with publishers/authors, and a worldwide audience would require territory-by-territory society licences or a worldwide publisher deal (multi-territory is typically only possible through publisher direct licensing).
- Operator is a Wyoming LLC: unclear which society/tariff applies; ZAiKS may treat it as a foreign service (excluded in calculator) or as Polish-resident controlled; needs confirmation by internet@zaiks.org.pl.
- No ZAiKS licence for a US LLC site was verified.

### Gaps
- Actual ZAiKS Table 13 rates (songs), and whether ZAiKS holds the reproduction/sync-type rights for such a video; whether a US LLC can contract with ZAiKS; territorial scope of ZAiKS online licences.

## 6. Practical alternatives and takedown/risk model

### Takeaway
Public-domain songs, own compositions, written permission from authors/publishers, and platform-hosted covers are the low-friction paths. On a self-hosted site, risk is takedown notices to Cloudflare/Hetzner/payment processors and (in the worst case) claims for damages.

### Cited Findings
- US: works first published in 1930 entered the public domain on 1 Jan 2026 ("Georgia on My Mind", "I Got Rhythm"); US only, terms differ elsewhere. (Prim.) — [Duke CSPD](https://web.law.duke.edu/cspd/publicdomainday/2026/)
- Poland: works of authors who died more than 70 years ago are free to use (no ZAiKS licence needed). (Sec.) — [rp.pl](https://www.rp.pl/opinie-prawne/art2751421-cover-a-prawo-autorskie-w-praktyce)
- DMCA agent designation: $6 flat fee, expires after 3 years, online filing at copyright.gov/dmca-directory. (Sec., 2016-17 law-firm notes) — [Greenberg Glusker](https://www.greenbergglusker.com/technology/publications/dont-lose-your-websites-safe-harbor-protection-under-the-dmca-new-procedures-for-registering-agents), [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/client-advisories/u-s-copyright-office-announces-important-changes-to-dmca-agent-designation.md)
- 512(c) safe harbour needs designated agent, repeat infringer policy. (Prim.) — [17 USC 512](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)
- Polish adaptation vs performance and interpretation boundaries — [rp.pl](https://www.rp.pl/opinie-prawne/art2751421-cover-a-prawo-autorskie-w-praktyce)

### Inferences
- EU/PL formula: public domain when author (for lyrics+music: the last surviving of composer and lyricist) died 70+ years ago; in 2026 that is authors who died in 1955 or earlier (my calculation; verify per song, incl. translators of Polish lyrics, arrangers of traditional songs, and the "performance" of a PD song by another artist is not a limitation for own recording). For a worldwide audience, the strictest relevant territory matters; the US rule is publication before 1931 for older works.
- Risk model on own site: no platform licence, no Content ID shares, takedown notices go to hosting (Hetzner), CDN (Cloudflare) and payment partners (Stripe may also care once paid access exists); DMCA agent registration protects only against third-party uploads, not his own content. Paid access increases exposure and the damages narrative (commercial benefit). (Inference.)
- Linking/embedding a YouTube-hosted cover (YouTube's licences) is the lowest-effort route for covers; hosting on own site needs own licences. (Inference; embedded YouTube players retain platform licences per PRS FAQ, which lists third-party platforms as outside LOML.)

### Gaps
- No source verified on Cloudflare/Hetzner abuse-handling procedure specifics.
- Platform licences for TikTok/Instagram/Spotify covers: not researched in sources; known only as general practice (unverified).

## 7. Recommendation and cost model

### Takeaway
Licensing covers for his own self-hosted site is feasible but is per-song negotiation, not a purchase button; for about 10 covers a year the realistic cost is low-thousands of dollars and weeks of email per song, with a real chance of refusal. A mixed strategy (PD/own/permissioned songs on the site; popular covers on YouTube with embedding) is the easy path.

### Cited Findings
- Cost anchors: mechanical via Easy Song $19.99/song plus royalties (audio only); sync quote-based: indie $50-$1,000, micro-sync $5-$500, majors "weeks or months"/no fixed price. — see sections 3-4.
- ZAiKS: fee as percentage of receipts if site becomes paid. — see section 5.

### Inferences
- Rough budget for 10 covers/year (my estimate, not sourced): $500-$2,000 if mostly indie publishers say yes; $3,000-$10,000+ if several majors are included; plus ZAiKS/PRO fees; plus 2-12 weeks per song; some refusals. Paid access later: re-negotiate or buy "all media, worldwide, perpetual, incl. subscription/paid" from the start.
- First steps: (1) list target songs and look up publisher/split share via ZAiKS/ASCAP/BMI/MLC search; (2) email Easy Song custom licensing and 2-3 publishers for a quote for one pilot song (state: own site, worldwide, free now, paid later, perpetual); (3) ask internet@zaiks.org.pl how a Wyoming LLC with Polish owner is treated; (4) meanwhile publish PD/own songs and post popular covers on YouTube.
- Mechanical-only products (Easy Song $19.99, Songfile) do not solve the video problem; do not buy them thinking they do.

### Gaps
- No verified end-to-end example of an individual obtaining sync for a cover for own-site use with price paid.
