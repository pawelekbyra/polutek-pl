# US sales tax for a Wyoming single-member LLC selling lifetime (one-time) streaming access to pre-recorded videos to US consumers (state of knowledge: Oct 2026)

Scope notes: US state/local sales tax only; federal income-tax is a short flag. Research was done via web search/fetch on 2026-10-12. Several state DOR pages (WA, UT, MD, TX, WY statute, Chicago) returned 403/404 to the fetcher, so many state-level claims rest on secondary summaries (Fonoa, Commenda, PwC/RSM/SCH, Avalara). Where secondary sources conflict, this is flagged. Nothing here is tax advice.

## 1. Economic nexus (Wayfair): per-state thresholds in 2026, dropped transaction tests, how thresholds are measured

### Takeaway
After South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018) every state with a sales tax (45 states + DC + PR; AK only locally) has an economic-nexus rule; the near-universal test is $100,000 of sales into the state, with $250k in AL/MS and $500k in CA/NY/TX. As of Aug 2026, 15 jurisdictions still keep a 200-transaction alternative; AK (2025), UT (Jul 2025), IL (Jan 2026), KY (Aug 2026), WY (Jul 2024), NC (Jul 2024) etc. have dropped it. What counts toward the threshold (gross vs retail vs taxable; marketplace sales) differs by state, so non-taxable sales can still trigger registration in "gross" states.

### Cited Findings
- Sales Tax Institute chart "as of 8/1/2026": nearly all states $100,000; Alabama $250,000 (no txn test); Mississippi >$250,000 (no txn test); California $500,000 (no txn test, removed Apr 2019); Texas $500,000 (no txn test); New York $500,000 AND more than 100 sales (four preceding sales-tax quarters). Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire, Oregon: no sales tax. — [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus)
- Transaction test still alive (alternative "or" 200 transactions) per STI/Kintsugi in: Arkansas, DC, Georgia, Hawaii, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, Nebraska, Nevada, New Jersey, Ohio, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia (15 "either"); Connecticut requires both $100k AND 200 transactions; NY requires both $500k AND 100 sales. — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md); [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus)
- Recently dropped the 200-transaction test: Alaska (local, Jan 1 2025), Utah (Jul 1 2025), Illinois (Jan 1 2026, Public Act 104-0006), Kentucky (Aug 1 2026, HB 757). Earlier: Wyoming (Jul 1 2024), North Carolina (Jul 1 2024), Indiana (Jan 1 2024), Louisiana (Aug 2023), South Dakota (Jul 2023), Wisconsin (Feb 2021), Maine (Jan 2022), Massachusetts, Washington, Colorado, Iowa, California and others earlier. — [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus); [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md)
- Measurement of "sales": STI defines Gross = all sales incl. resale/exempt; Retail = excl. resale; Taxable = excl. non-taxable. Taxable-only counting: Arkansas, Florida, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Missouri (taxable tangible), Tennessee/Illinois etc. count retail. Gross counting (incl. exempt sales): AZ, CA, HI, ID, IN, IA, KS, KY, ME, MD, MA, MI, NJ, NC, SC, SD, TX, UT, VT, WA, WV, WI, WY. — [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus)
- Stripe warns some jurisdictions (e.g., Arizona, Indiana, North Carolina) count non-taxable sales toward the threshold, so a seller of only non-taxable goods may still have to register and file a zero/information return. — [Stripe Docs: Monitor your obligations](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring)
- Marketplace sales: treatment varies. Included in the seller's own threshold in e.g. CA, HI, ID, IA, KS, KY, MD, MI, NJ, NC, SC, SD, TX, VT, WA, WV; excluded in AZ, CO, FL, IN, ME, MA, WY, etc.; excluded only if the marketplace collects in AL, GA, IL, OK, PA, TN, UT (Kintsugi) — STI shows some differences (e.g., it lists PA as "gross across all channels incl. marketplace"; Kintsugi says excluded if marketplace collects). Direct sales via your own site are always yours. — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md); [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus)
- Measurement windows: mostly "current or previous calendar year"; rolling 12 months in IL, MO, TN, TX, PA, MS, NY (four quarters), CT (12 months ending Sep 30); collection start ranges from next transaction to the 1st of the third month after crossing. — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md)
- Grace/collection-start rules per state (examples): WY, OH, GA, ID, AR, CA, UT, SD "when threshold exceeded"; WA 1st of month 2 months after; PA 1st of month 90 days after; TX 1st of month 4 months after; TN 3 months; AL/FL/MI/NM/RI start next Jan 1. — [Stripe Docs: Use Stripe to register (US)](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/united-states)
- Late registration: register with an effective date back to when nexus began (back-pay + penalty waivers) or use a Voluntary Disclosure Agreement; lookback often 3 years vs 5. — [Stripe Docs: Use Stripe to register (US)](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/united-states)
- Chicago amusement tax has its own enforcement safe harbor: out-of-state sellers with under $100,000 revenue from Chicago customers in the last four calendar quarters are not expected to collect (policy, not statute). — [Beancount.io (secondary, low-authority)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/13/chicago-amusement-tax-2026-venues-streamers-ticket-sellers-guide); Stripe also notes a $100k Chicago amusement threshold — [Stripe Docs: Illinois](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states/illinois)
- Source discrepancies: Kintsugi lists Alaska as "$100,000, local tax only" while TaxCloud lists a general $100,000. Kintsugi lists Arizona/Massachusetts/Wyoming as sales-only (agrees with STI). — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md); [TaxCloud](https://taxcloud.com/blog/sales-tax-nexus-by-state/)

### Inferences
- Because thresholds are per state, a small catalog business will cross only in its biggest customer states first (CA/NY/TX have $500k thresholds, so FL, PA, OH, IL, WA, GA, NC, NJ, MA are likelier first trips at $100k).
- Revenue, not profit, is measured; refunds are netted by Stripe monitoring.
- Wayfair itself was not separately fetched; background fact (physical presence rule overturned June 2018) is widely known and assumed.

### Gaps
- No primary DOR confirmation of each state's threshold (relied on STI table dated 8/1/2026 and Kintsugi); a few details (STI vs Kintsugi on marketplace treatment) differ.
- Did not research gross-receipts taxes (WA B&O, OR CAT, OH CAT, TX franchise, NV commerce tax) that can have separate economic nexus.

## 2. Taxability of streaming video / VOD / "lifetime access" in key states

### Takeaway
A majority of the large digital-tax states tax streamed video regardless of whether it is a subscription or a one-time purchase (WA, PA, OH, TN, CT, MD, TX [via cable/amusement classification], UT from Jul 2026, ME from Jan 2026, CO, HI, MN, NE, AR, LA, NC, SD, VT, DC). Exempt or generally not taxed: NY, MA, CA, FL (with CST caveat), IL (state; Chicago amusement tax applies to streaming), VA, NV, MI, MO, ND, OK, and in most of those a download is also untaxed. Four states (WY, ID, IN, GA) tax digital audio-visual works only when the buyer gets "permanent" use — a "lifetime access" product is a candidate for being treated as permanent there. The "downloaded vs streamed" distinction rarely helps: almost every state that taxes downloads also taxes streaming (WA, PA, CO explicit), while Chicago and NY draw lines the other way (Chicago taxes streaming but not permanent downloads; NY taxes neither).

### Cited Findings
**Statutory / agency-level (best evidence)**
- Washington: sales/use tax applies to all digital products regardless of how accessed (download, streaming, subscription); DOR says it does not matter whether the purchaser gets permanent or nonpermanent right of use; digital automated services also taxed and subject to retailing B&O. — [WA DOR digital products (via search summary)](https://dor.wa.gov/node/37916); [WA DOR PDF](https://dor.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2022-02/DigitalProductTaxation.pdf)
- Pennsylvania: 6% state tax (+1% Allegheny, +2% Philadelphia) applies to digital video that is downloaded or streamed, including a Netflix/Hulu-type subscription; sale sourced to customer's billing address on file; tax applies to individual purchases, subscriptions or other arrangements; no tax on sales to customers outside PA. — [PA DOR digital products](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/sales-use-and-hotel-occupancy-tax/digital-products); [PA DOR Q&A PDF](https://www.revenue.pa.gov:443/TaxTypes/SUT/Documents/digital_products_tax_qa.pdf)
- Connecticut: since Oct 1 2019 "digital goods" (audio, visual and audio-visual works incl. movies, videos, entertainment programs, accessed or transferred electronically, incl. streamed, subscription, in-app) are taxed at 6.35% (previously 1%); presumption of delivery in CT if billing address in CT. — [RSM summary of DRS SN 2019(8)](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2019/important-connecticut-sales-tax-changes-for-digital-goods.html); [DRS Special Notice 2019(8)](https://test-cd.ct.gov/-/media/drs/publications/pubssn/2019/sn-2019(8).pdf)
- Maryland: 6% sales tax on "digital products" incl. downloads or streams of motion pictures, music videos, TV, tutorials; includes subscriptions to access content; destination sourcing; effective Mar 2021 (HB 932 veto override; amended by SB 787). — [SC&H Group](https://www.schgroup.com/insights/blog/tax/maryland-enacts-new-sales-tax-on-digital-goods-and-services/); [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/maryland-expands-tax-on-digital-goods)
- Ohio: SSUTA-style definitions since Jan 1 2014: "specified digital product" = electronically transferred digital audiovisual works, audio works, books; taxable for permanent or less-than-permanent use, whether or not continued payment is required; Netflix-type subscriptions taxed (but not video programming in cable packages). — [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/ohio-amends-data-center-exemption-and-adopts-digital-products-definitions); [search summary of Ohio reporting](https://www.fox19.com/story/22698983/changes-to-ohio-budget-could-add-sales-tax-to-digital-products)
- Colorado: Rule 39-26-102(15) (eff. Jan 30 2021) and HB 21-1312 (eff. Jul 1 2021) treat digital goods as tangible personal property; delivery method irrelevant; examples include a one-time purchase of a movie accessed in a browser without saving a copy, and a monthly subscription for streaming. Netflix case settled Jul 22 2026 and Court of Appeals ruling (taxable) stands. — [Avalara, Jul 2026](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2026/07/netflix-colorado-sales-tax-on-streaming-services.html)
- Utah: SB 162 signed Mar 23 2026, effective Jul 1 2026: sales/use tax on amounts paid for access to digital audiovisual works, digital audio works, digital books and gaming services, including streaming and subscription, even without download; excludes transactions already under the Multi-Channel Video or Audio Service Tax Act. — [LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/united-states/ut-sb162-digital-products); [VATCalc summary (search result)](https://www.vatcalc.com/united-states/us-utah-sales-tax-on-digital-services-july-2026); [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/state-local-tax/newsletters/salt-insights/assets/pwc-utah-clarifies-sales-tax-on-streaming-and-prewritten-software.pdf) (PwC PDF not fetched; listed in search results)
- Maine: from Jan 1 2026 the 5.5% sales tax extended to digital services/streaming (Netflix, Hulu, Spotify); LD 210 also repeals the old Service Provider Tax. — [VATCalc (search result snippet; page itself returned 403)](https://www.vatcalc.com/united-states/us-maine-extends-sales-tax-on-digital-and-streaming/)
- Texas: Comptroller 2018 private letter ruling treated a streaming video service as taxable "cable television service" (entire lump-sum charge); amended data-processing rule keeps streaming video out of "data processing" but taxable as cable TV; video-game streaming taxable as amusement; private ruling binds only the requester. Some guides say pure streaming with no download isn't taxable — conflict. — [DDH Tax summary](https://ddhtax.com/?p=4238); [Grant Thornton](https://grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2025/salt/p-t/tx-updates-data-processing-services-tax-rule-04-11); [Ezel ruling digest](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/tx/201801006l-essence-of-the-transaction-combined-services-taxable-transaction-vs)
- New York: electronically delivered music/photos/video files are intangible, not taxable (TSB-A rulings 2007, 2008); prewritten software (incl. remote access) is taxable; live/real-time content and bundling with taxable software can pull the whole charge into tax. — [Ezel TSB-A-07(11)S](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/ny/tsb-a-07-11s-issue-raised-by-petitioner-is-whether-the-sale-of-a-video-delivered); [Ezel TSB-A-08(22)S](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/ny/tsb-a-08-22s-issue-raised-by-petitioner-is-whether-the-sale-of-a-video-delivered); official tax.ny.gov bulletin not retrieved
- Illinois/Chicago: state ROT does not reach digital downloads/streaming; City of Chicago amusement tax (Municipal Code ch. 4-156) applies to electronically delivered amusements (video/audio streaming, games) based on customer's Chicago location regardless of seller's location; permanent downloads were not targeted (2015 ruling). Rate: 9% originally; one 2026 guide says 10.25% "since Jan 1, 2025" and another says "since January" — not verified against ordinance. Stripe Tax threshold monitoring does NOT monitor amusement-tax thresholds. — [Skadden 2015](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2015/07/city-of-chicago-expands-tax-reach-to-internet-serv); [Beancount.io 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/13/chicago-amusement-tax-2026-venues-streamers-ticket-sellers-guide); [Stripe monitoring doc](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring)
- Hawaii: General Excise Tax (4% state + 0.5%–0.7% county surcharge) is on the seller's gross receipts; downloads/streaming video generally taxable; nexus $100,000 OR 200 transactions; Hawaii registration fee $20 (Stripe) vs "free" (a guide). — [Commenda Hawaii](https://www.commenda.io/global-indirect-tax/guides/hawaii-sales-tax); [Kintsugi Hawaii](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/hawaii.md); [Stripe registration doc](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/united-states)
- Wyoming statute: taxes "specified digital products" (electronically transferred digital audio-visual works, audio works, books) only if the purchaser has permanent use (HB0029, 2010); proposed streaming/subscription tax in 2021 HB0176 and 2024 HB0022 did not become law — HB0022 "Failed Introduction 1-59-2-0-0" on 2024-02-12. — [WY Legislature HB0029 summary](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2010/Summaries/HB0029.htm) (note: the fetch tool's summary mislabeled this as "Indiana"; the URL is wyoleg.gov); [LegiScan HB0022](https://www.legiscan.com/WY/research/HB0022); [WY HB0022 fiscal note](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2024/Fiscal/HB0022.pdf)

**Downloaded vs streamed vs "lifetime"**
- Several states explicitly say the delivery method does not matter (WA: downloaded, streamed, subscription; CO: delivery method doesn't change taxability; PA: downloaded or streamed). — [WA DOR](https://dor.wa.gov/node/37916); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2026/07/netflix-colorado-sales-tax-on-streaming-services.html); [PA DOR](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/sales-use-and-hotel-occupancy-tax/digital-products)
- Some states tax only with a permanent right: Idaho, Indiana, Wyoming (Fonoa named these three; Georgia per another source). — [Fonoa](https://www.fonoa.com/resources/blog/guide-to-the-taxability-of-digital-products-in-the-us)
- Idaho: leases, rentals, subscriptions not taxable; taxed only with permanent right of use. — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/sales-tax/sales-tax-on-digital-goods-by-state)
- Ohio taxes both permanent and less-than-permanent use "regardless of whether continued payment is required." — [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/ohio-amends-data-center-exemption-and-adopts-digital-products-definitions)

### Inferences
- A "lifetime access to stream" product is a streamed (not downloaded) product; where streaming is taxed, treat as taxable; where only "permanent use" is taxed (WY, ID, IN, GA), "lifetime" access is an indefinite-duration right (SSUTA-style "permanent" = indefinite) and so is more likely to be taxable than a monthly subscription. No state primary source on the lifetime-streaming edge case was found; this is an inference.
- A one-time purchase (vs subscription) does not escape tax in WA, PA, OH, CT, MD, CO, UT: the statutes tax sale/access of the product irrespective of payment structure.
- Best candidates to be "not taxable but still nexus-relevant" are NY, MA, CA, IL (outside Chicago): sales there still count toward gross-sales thresholds in some states (e.g., CA $500k gross; MA $100k gross).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch primary DOR pages for TN, TX, NY, MD, UT, HI, OH; relied on secondary sources and rulings digests.
- Fonoa vs Commenda conflicts on RI, NJ, KY, FL, ME (Commenda stale); status of many "taxable per Fonoa only" states not verified.
- No authoritative source on how "lifetime" (perpetual but revocable/non-transferable) access is classified under SSUTA "permanent" definitions; no state guidance specific to streamed lifetime access found.
- Chicago amusement tax current rate (9% vs 10.25%) unverified against the ordinance; ITFA challenge status not checked.
- Local-level taxes (Colorado home-rule cities, Alabama, Louisiana, Alaska) not researched.

## 3. Wyoming itself

### Takeaway
Wyoming is the LLC's formation state but is also a state with a 4% state rate and local add-ons; digital audio-visual works are taxable only when the buyer has permanent use (lifetime access plausibly qualifies), and streaming/subscription taxation bills have failed. Wyoming has an economic-nexus rule ($100,000, 200-transaction test removed Jul 1 2024) and a one-time $60 license; Wyoming levies no state income tax (not independently sourced in this session).

### Cited Findings
- Wyoming imposes sales tax on "specified digital products" only if purchaser has permanent use (enrolled-bill summary for 2010 HB0029). — [WY Legislature](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2010/Summaries/HB0029.htm)
- Streaming/subscription tax proposals (2021 HB0176, 2023 interim committee draft, 2024 HB0022) did not become law; HB0022 failed introduction 2024-02-12; its fiscal note estimated ~$5.8M/yr. A later syfert.com copy of 39-15-103 shows the permanent-use rule without the streaming sentence (third-party, unverified). — [LegiScan](https://www.legiscan.com/WY/research/HB0022); [HB0176 (2021)](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2021/Introduced/HB0176.pdf); [HB0022 introduced](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2024/Introduced/HB0022.pdf); [Syfert 39-15-103](https://syfert.com/wyoming/sections/39-15-103.html)
- Remote-seller rule effective Feb 1 2019; threshold $100,000 gross sales in current or prior calendar year; 200-transaction test removed effective Jul 1 2024; marketplace-facilitated sales excluded; sales-tax license $60 (Stripe also lists Wyoming fee as $60); register via WYIFS/Department of Revenue Excise Tax Division. — [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2018/11/wyoming-to-tax-remote-sales-starting-february-2019.html); [Stripe registration doc](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/united-states); [Commenda Wyoming](https://www.commenda.io/usa/wyoming/sales-tax-permit)
- State rate 4%; counties may add local sales tax (a guide says 0-4%; combined typically 4-6%; resort districts up to 8%); Tax Foundation midyear 2026 notes Wyoming was the only state whose combined rate fell vs January. Wyoming is a Streamlined Sales Tax member since Jan 1 2008 (secondary). — [Tax Foundation midyear 2026](https://taxfoundation.org/data/all/state/2026-sales-tax-rates-midyear/); [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/wyoming-sales-and-use-tax-registration-guide-for-businesses); [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/usa/wyoming/sales-tax-permit)
- Wyoming sales-tax data: Commenda's table lists Wyoming 4.00% state / 5.44% combined average and "permanent-use criterion applies". — [Commenda digital goods](https://www.commenda.io/sales-tax/sales-tax-on-digital-goods-by-state)
- Conflicting vendor claims: PayProGlobal-type guides say SaaS not taxed in Wyoming; others still print the old $100k-or-200-transactions test (stale). — [Commenda SaaS Wyoming](https://www.commenda.io/usa/wyoming/is-saas-taxable); [Quaderno](https://quaderno.io/guides/wyoming/sales-tax/registration/)

### Inferences
- A seller permit is only *required* in Wyoming once Wyoming-destination sales exceed $100k (as remote seller) or if the LLC has physical presence in Wyoming; a registered agent address alone is generally treated as not a physical-presence nexus by most states, but this was not verified for Wyoming. Because the LLC is formed in Wyoming, the Department could in principle view it as a Wyoming taxpayer; the cheapest assumption is that Wyoming-destination sales are sourced like any other state's (destination) and the permit is only needed above $100k, subject to a CPA/WY DOR confirmation.
- "No state income tax in Wyoming" is background knowledge, not sourced this session; for a foreign-owned disregarded LLC the relevant income tax question is federal (section 4), not Wyoming.

### Gaps
- Wyoming DOR primary pages (revenue.wyo.gov) and current W.S. 39-15-103/39-16-103 text could not be retrieved (403); wyoming-specific guidance on remote vs in-state status for a Wyoming-registered entity with a registered agent only was not found.
- Whether HB0126 (2025) or any 2026 bill changed digital-product treatment not confirmed.

## 4. Federal tax flag (foreign-owned disregarded single-member LLC)

### Takeaway
A single-member LLC wholly owned by a non-US person is a "foreign-owned US disregarded entity" and must file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 (paper/fax only) when it has reportable transactions; the base penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per form, with an additional $25,000 per 30-day period if it continues more than 90 days after IRS notice. Whether the LLC has effectively connected income (ECI)/US trade or business is a separate question not researched.

### Cited Findings
- Foreign-owned US DEs are "reporting corporations" that must file Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 (write "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" across the top), by the Form 1120 due date including extensions; cannot e-file; mailed to Ogden, UT or faxed (855-887-7737); extension via Form 7004. — [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)
- Penalty: $25,000 for failure to file when due; substantially incomplete form counts as failure; additional $25,000 per related party per 30-day period after 90 days from IRS notification; criminal penalties under sections 7203, 7206, 7207 may apply. — [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)
- Treas. Reg. §1.6038A-1 extended the requirement to foreign-owned DEs for tax years beginning on/after Jan 1, 2017; filing needed even for dormant LLCs with reportable transactions such as owner capital contributions (secondary tax-firm sources). — [IRS Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472); [SDO CPA](https://www.sdocpa.com/form-5472-foreign-owned-business-filing/)

### Inferences
- Owner contributions/distributions to the LLC are typically the reportable transactions; the LLC will likely have to file annually regardless of sales-tax status.
- US state sales-tax compliance is independent of the federal return; filing Form 5472 does not register anything for sales tax.

### Gaps
- ECI/US trade-or-business analysis (US-source vs foreign-source sales of digital content by a foreign-owned LLC with no US presence), treaty effect (Poland-US), EIN/ITIN issues: not researched; refer to CPA.

## 5. SST, Stripe Tax / TaxJar / Avalara, merchant of record, realistic compliance approach

### Takeaway
Cheapest realistic path: don't register anywhere until a state threshold is actually approached; use Stripe's built-in threshold monitoring (free in both Stripe Tax plans, ~$10k revenue trigger) plus a manual checklist (taxable-vs-gross state tests, Chicago, Texas $500k, etc.), then register state-by-state only when crossed (state fees $0-$100; Wyoming $60), and either self-file or use Tax Complete/TaxJar/SST CSP. A Merchant of Record (Paddle ~5% + 50c; Lemon Squeezy ~5% + 50c; Stripe Managed Payments 3.5% on top of Stripe fees) removes sales-tax liability from the LLC but costs more per sale and may restrict sellers/product types.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe Tax threshold monitoring: tracks Stripe-processed sales (plus Stripe Tax API/off-platform invoices imported) by customer location, compared to local thresholds; alerts at $10,000 USD of prior-year revenue; email within 1-2 days; new transactions added within 7 days; limitations: assumes preset tax code and all sales taxable at destination, cannot differentiate retail/wholesale or marketplace, does not monitor amusement/parking/retail-delivery taxes (Chicago amusement tax), doesn't monitor your home state; US transactions need state or postal code for attribution. — [Stripe Docs: Monitor](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring)
- Stripe Tax Basic: no monthly fee; 0.5% per transaction (no-code: Checkout, Billing, Invoicing, Payment Links) where registered; API 2 zł (~$0.50) per transaction incl. 10 calculation calls; includes threshold monitoring, calculation, collection; registration/filing not included. — [Stripe Tax pricing (PL-localized)](https://stripe.com/tax/pricing); [Dodo Payments summary of US pricing](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-tax-explained)
- Stripe Tax Complete: monthly subscription "starting at 360 zł per month", 1-year contract, includes registrations, calculations and filings (US filing via TaxJar in select states); tiers 360/1,700/4,000/6,000 zł. — [Stripe Tax pricing (PL-localized)](https://stripe.com/tax/pricing)
- Stripe "Register for me" (Tax Complete only): remote sellers only; foreign-owned single-member LLC needs EIN + one SSN/ITIN for owner; US bank account required in fee-charging states; typical completion ≤30 days (60 in Dec–Feb); state fees: AZ $12+, CO $16+$50 deposit, CT $100, HI $20, IN $25, NV $15 + deposit, SC $50, WA $50, WV $30, WI $20, WY $60; registration is separate from filing (self-file, filing partner, or Stripe automated filing). — [Stripe Docs: Use Stripe to register (US)](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/united-states)
- SST: free "CSP-compensated seller" services in member states for sellers who register through the SST central registration system; the CSP label only determines the provider's compensation, not whether you must collect; certified providers list; some certified systems not currently offering free services. A vendor page says the SST has 24 member states. — [SSTGB free services](https://streamlinedsalestax.org/for-businesses/free-sales-tax-services); [SSTGB CSP definitions](https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/certified-service-providers/freeservices/volunteer-definitions); [Sovos (vendor)](https://sovos.com/content-library/sut/model-1-sst-sales-tax-compliance/)
- Merchant of Record: Lemon Squeezy says it is "merchant of record for all sales through our platform", deducts tax from payout, and "we are liable for all of the complicated bits" — the page does not mention US states; Lemon Squeezy was acquired by Stripe (2024). — [Lemon Squeezy docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/sales-tax-vat); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/?p=2018528)
- Paddle: says it registers, charges and remits sales tax in 100+ jurisdictions and, in its blog, handles "all U.S. states"; price 5% + 50c per transaction (third-party comparisons; verify on Paddle pricing page). — [Paddle tax & compliance](https://paddle.com/platform/tax-and-compliance); [Paddle blog](https://paddle.com/blog/states-with-no-sales-tax); [Dodo Payments comparison](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy/)
- Stripe Managed Payments (Stripe's MoR): public preview in Feb 2026 API, Checkout/Payment Links support Apr 22 2026; fee 3.5% per successful transaction on the full amount (incl. taxes) in addition to standard Stripe processing fees; includes "global indirect tax handling (calculation and collection, and — in supported countries — filing and remittance)" in 75+ countries, fraud, disputes, support; digital products (SaaS, software, digital content) eligible; seller-location eligibility list not retrieved. — [Stripe support: Managed Payments pricing](https://support.stripe.com/questions/managed-payments-pricing); [Stripe changelog](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/dahlia/2026-04-22/managed-payments)
- Commenda (vendor with a commercial interest) argues sellers using a MoR still have US exposure considerations; treat as marketing. — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/?p=1246928)

### Inferences
- With a Wyoming single-member LLC, the cheapest compliant approach is: (1) enable Stripe Tax Basic monitoring (free) and collect billing state/ZIP at checkout so US revenue is attributed to states; (2) set a calendar check at ~50-70% of $100k per state (taxable-sales states) and for gross states count all US sales; (3) register only on crossing (Wyoming $60 + WA $50 + CT $100 etc. are low), starting with the first-crossed states; (4) use Stripe Tax Complete/TaxJar or SST CSP only once 2+ states are active; (5) get a CPA for the first Voluntary Disclosure/lookback if a state is crossed late.
- A MoR is economically attractive only when the number of states crossed would be large or admin time is the constraint; at 5% + 50c on low-priced access products the fee can be higher than DIY compliance until revenue is high. A MoR also does not remove the federal Form 5472 obligation of the LLC.
- Stripe's monitoring blind spot for Chicago amusement tax and the NY/TX $500k/100-sale joint tests means a manual check is needed even if using Stripe.

- USD price for Tax Complete tiers not retrieved (page localized to PLN); Paddle official fee and US-state registration list not confirmed from primary Paddle docs; Lemon Squeezy's current post-Stripe status/state list not confirmed; whether Paddle/Lemon Squeezy/Stripe MP accept a Wyoming LLC with a Polish owner and video-content products (content restrictions) not researched.
- Avalara/TaxJar standalone pricing not retrieved.

## 6. Rates and sourcing

### Takeaway
Digital goods are sourced to the buyer's location (destination), so the state + county + city rate where the buyer is located applies; population-weighted average combined state+local rate is 7.53% (Tax Foundation, July 2026), with the highest at 9.5-10.1% (LA, TN, WA, AR, AL); no tax in OR/MT/NH/DE and AK only local.

### Cited Findings
- Tax Foundation midyear 2026 (Jul 1 2026): population-weighted average combined state+local sales tax rate 7.53%; top five: Louisiana 10.13%, Tennessee 9.61%, Washington 9.57%, Arkansas 9.48%, Alabama 9.46%; Alaska local-only avg 1.82%; Wyoming the only state with a combined-rate reduction since January. — [Tax Foundation midyear 2026](https://taxfoundation.org/data/all/state/2026-sales-tax-rates-midyear/)
- Stripe Tax assumes all sales are taxable at the destination and attributes location by validated address, customer address, AVS ZIP, card-issuer country, payment method, IP. — [Stripe Docs: Monitor](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring)
- Examples of state-level sourcing: PA uses customer billing address on file; CT presumes delivery in CT if billing address in CT; Maryland uses destination-based sourcing. — [PA DOR](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/sales-use-and-hotel-occupancy-tax/digital-products); [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2019/important-connecticut-sales-tax-changes-for-digital-goods.html); [SC&H](https://www.schgroup.com/insights/blog/tax/maryland-enacts-new-sales-tax-on-digital-goods-and-services/)
- State rates (state / avg combined per Commenda page, dated; use Tax Foundation for current): WA 6.50/9.38, TX 6.25/8.20, NY 4.00/8.52, PA 6.00/6.34, OH 5.75/7.24, TN 7.00/9.55, IL 6.25/8.86, CT 6.35/6.35, MD 6.00/6.00, MA 6.25/6.25, UT 4.85/7.25, HI 4.00/4.50, WY 4.00/5.44. — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/sales-tax/sales-tax-on-digital-goods-by-state)
- Connecticut digital goods 6.35%; Maine 5.5% digital; Pennsylvania 6% (+1% Allegheny, +2% Philadelphia); Chicago amusement 9%/10.25% (unverified). — [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2019/important-connecticut-sales-tax-changes-for-digital-goods.html); [VATCalc Maine](https://www.vatcalc.com/united-states/us-maine-extends-sales-tax-on-digital-and-streaming/); [PA DOR](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/sales-use-and-hotel-occupancy-tax/digital-products)

### Inferences
- A US consumer price of, e.g., $100 in WA effectively means ~$9.5 added at checkout (tax-exclusive pricing) or ~8.7% absorbed if prices are tax-inclusive — pricing policy needs to be decided state by state.
- Home-rule/local jurisdictions (Colorado, Chicago, Alabama, Louisiana, Alaska) can impose separate collection obligations; the Stripe/SST/CSP tools handle this only in part.
